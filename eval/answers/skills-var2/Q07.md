**Not steadily. Over the five fiscal years that can be compared fairly, Montréal's ER strain peaked in 2022-2023 and then eased back to roughly its 2021-2022 level by 2025-2026. This year's numbers aren't directly comparable yet.**

Montréal region, MSSS cumulative ER file, each year cumulated over the same window (April 1 → period 7, roughly mid-October):

| Fiscal year (April 1 → period 7) | ERs in the data | Mean stretcher stay | Stretcher visits 24 h+ | Stretcher visits 48 h+ | Mean delay to first physician |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 21 | 18.0 h | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2022-2023 | 21 | 19.9 h | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2023-2024 | 21 | 19.2 h | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2.7 h |
| 2024-2025 | 21 | 18.7 h | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2025-2026 | 21 | 18.4 h | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2026-2027 (so far, see below) | 17 | 19.0 h | 23.9% | 7.3% | 2.7 h |

How I read it (lower is better for every indicator):

- **2021-2022 to 2025-2026, the same 21 ERs each year:** the mean stretcher stay rose from 18.0 h to 19.9 h in 2022-2023, then fell back each year to 18.4 h. The share of stretcher visits lasting 24 h or more went from 25.2% up to 27.3%, then down to 24.6%. So overcrowding worsened into 2022-2023 and has improved since, rather than getting steadily worse.
- **2026-2027 so far:** the stretcher stay is 0.55 h longer than at the same point last year and the 48 h+ share is up 0.6 points, while the 24 h+ share is down 0.7 points. But only 17 Montréal ERs are in this year's data, versus 21 last year (4 fewer), so the region's totals aren't comparable with earlier years. The live ERs with no 2026-2027 row in this file are Hôpital du Sacré-Coeur-de-Montréal, Hôpital Fleury, Hôpital Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost, likely the same set, though I haven't confirmed it at the facility level.
- **Today**, Montréal's stretcher-occupancy rate is 161% (MSSS snapshot published 2026-11-05 at 08:45), against 130% for Québec as a whole. One snapshot says nothing about a trend, and the hourly archive behind occupancy history only goes back to 2026-11-03, too short to compare.

These are averages over months of past visits. They describe how Montréal's ERs have been performing, not how long a visit today would take. The mean delay to a physician covers all triage priorities.

_Source: MSSS cumulative ER file (Données cumulatives des urgences), fiscal years 2021-2022 to 2026-2027, each cumulated April 1 → period 7; current rate from the MSSS hourly ER file, snapshot published 2026-11-05 08:45._
