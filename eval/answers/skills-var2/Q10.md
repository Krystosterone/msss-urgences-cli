**Yes, by its own standard this year, Maisonneuve-Rosemont's ER is under more strain than usual: patients who left yesterday stayed noticeably longer than its 2026-2027 average. It isn't among Montréal's most crowded ERs at this moment, though.**

- **Stretcher patients** who left yesterday stayed 29.4 h on average (29:25), 33% longer than its 22.1 h mean so far this fiscal year (2026-2027, April 1 → period 7). That's noticeably longer than usual.
- **Walk-in (ambulatory) patients:** 7.8 h vs 5.8 h, +35%, also noticeably longer than usual.
- **Right now** (MSSS snapshot taken at 08:00, published 2026-11-05 at 08:45, freshly fetched): 133% stretcher occupancy (72 occupied / 54 functional), 99 patients present, 30 waiting to see a physician, and 31 on a stretcher for over 24 h, 8 of them over 48 h.
- **Against the rest of Montréal:** Montréal's rate (MSSS's regional total) is 161% and the mean of its ERs 152%, so HMR sits below that mean (Δ −19). In short: crowded, but less than many Montréal ERs right now, and with longer stays than it usually has.
- **Context:** HMR's norm this year is itself worse than last year's. So far in 2026-2027, 36.6% of its stretcher visits lasted 24 h or more, vs 28.5% at the same point in 2025-2026.
- **Hourly history** can't help yet: the archive only began on 2026-11-03 at 22:45, and around 8h there is a single other snapshot (142.6%) to compare this morning with. Too thin to say whether this hour is unusual.

Note what "today" means here: the stay comparison is about patients who left *yesterday*, and the live head counts have no fiscal-year equivalent. None of this predicts how long someone arriving now would wait. Patients are seen by triage priority, and for an estimate Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) is the official source.

_Sources: MSSS hourly ER file, snapshot of 08:00 published 2026-11-05 08:45 (not cached), joined with MSSS's cumulative ER file (2026-2027 through period 7)._
