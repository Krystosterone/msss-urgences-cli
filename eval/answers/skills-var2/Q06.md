**The data can't tell you which ER will see you fastest, or how long you'd wait. And for a sprain you may not need an ER at all.**

**Options for a sprain you'd call minor**

- **Info-Santé 811** (a nurse, 24/7) can tell you whether the ankle needs an exam or an X-ray, and where to go.
- **A pharmacist** can advise on pain relief and care for a minor sprain.
- **A clinic:** your own family doctor or GMF, Rendez-vous santé Québec, or the GAP (Québec.ca or 811) if you have no family doctor.

Go to an ER (call 911 if it seems serious) if you can't put any weight on the foot, the ankle looks deformed, the foot is numb, cold or pale, there's an open wound, or the pain and swelling are severe.

**How long would you wait?** The MSSS open data has no wait time. Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows, for each ER, an estimated wait before seeing a physician for a non-priority case. That estimate depends on the staff and the patients present when you arrive, doesn't apply to urgent cases, and isn't a promise. I can't compute one from the counts below.

- Left out because they serve specific patients: CHU Sainte-Justine and the Montreal Children's (children), Hôpital Douglas and the Institut universitaire en santé mentale de Montréal (psychiatry), and the Institut de cardiologie de Montréal (cardiology).
- **What these numbers can't say:** a lower occupancy or fewer people waiting doesn't mean you'll be seen sooner. Patients are seen by triage priority, and MSSS warns the counts don't show staffing and can change quickly. If you do go to an ER, the closest one is a reasonable choice: going farther for a lower number isn't a reliable way to be seen sooner.
