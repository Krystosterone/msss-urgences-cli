**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is much more crowded than Sainte-Justine's.**

MSSS snapshot taken at 08:00, published 2026-11-05 at 08:45 (about half an hour old, freshly fetched):

| | CHU Sainte-Justine | Montreal Children's (Glen site) |
|---|---|---|
| Stretcher occupancy | 88% (14 occupied / 16 functional) | 183% (22 occupied / 12 functional) |
| Patients present | 33 | 48 |
| Waiting to see a physician | 9 | 21 |
| On a stretcher over 24 h / over 48 h | 0 / 0 | 3 / 1 |
| Gap vs the mean of Montréal's ERs (152%) | Δ −64 | Δ +31 |
| Yesterday's stretcher stay vs its own norm this year | 6.6 h vs 6.4 h (+2%) | 10.3 h vs 8.8 h (+16%) |

**What each indicator means**

- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied ÷ functional stretchers (computed from MSSS's counts). It shows how full the stretcher area is. Above 100% means patients are on overflow stretchers, which MSSS counts as occupied but not as functional: the Children's has more children on stretchers than it has functional stretchers. It doesn't measure the waiting room, and it doesn't say how fast anyone is seen.
- **Patients present**: every patient registered in the ER at that moment, on a stretcher or in the waiting room.
- **Waiting to see a physician**: children present who haven't been assessed by a doctor yet, at every triage priority. It isn't a place in line: the sickest children are seen first, whenever they arrived.
- **Over 24 h / 48 h**: children who have been on a stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Gap vs the Montréal mean**: the difference from the plain mean of all Montréal ERs, adult ones included (MSSS's own Montréal rate is 161%). Comparing the two pediatric ERs with each other is more meaningful than comparing either with adult ERs.
- **Yesterday's stay vs its norm**: the average time from arrival to departure for patients who left yesterday, set against the same ER's average since April 1 (fiscal 2026-2027, through period 7). Sainte-Justine's is in line with its usual. The Children's is somewhat longer than usual, for stretcher patients (+16%) and for walk-in patients alike (5.2 h vs 4.5 h, +16%).

**What the numbers can't tell you:** they are head counts at one moment and change quickly. MSSS itself warns that fewer patients present or waiting doesn't mean a child will be seen sooner: the data shows nothing about staffing or about how urgent each case is, so it can't predict a wait at either hospital. For advice on whether to come in at all, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7; if a child's symptoms seem serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.

_Source: MSSS hourly ER file, snapshot of 08:00 published 2026-11-05 08:45 (not cached); fiscal-year-to-date values from MSSS's cumulative ER file, 2026-2027 through period 7._
