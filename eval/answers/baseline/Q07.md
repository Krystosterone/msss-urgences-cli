Broadly yes. Montréal has had chronic ER overcrowding for years and has consistently done worse than the Québec average. The trend over the last couple of years is mixed, with sharp winter peaks. Based on Santé Québec figures reported in the news (I couldn't access the official dataset directly):
- **Length of stay on a stretcher, Montréal region:** in Feb 4–17, 2025 it rose to **23.6 h, from 20.7 h a year earlier**. Province-wide the same period went from 17.8 h to 19.4 h. Another Santé Québec release cited Montréal going from 19.5 h to 23.2 h; the period wasn't specified.
- **Stretcher occupancy:** Montréal was at about **140% at the 2025 winter peak, up 16% from the year before**. Santé Québec listed several Montréal hospitals, including the Montreal General, among those where both occupancy and length of stay worsened between 2023-24 and 2024-25.
- **Late December 2025:** the 7-day average stretcher stay in Greater Montréal was **over 19 h**, versus more than 16 h province-wide.
- **Fiscal year 2025-26:** the provincial average stretcher stay was about **16 h 46 min**. The Lakeshore General (West Island) had the worst in Québec, at **30 h 54 min**. That's a bit better than the 2024-25 province-wide figures, so the most recent year may show slight improvement, but Montréal remains well above the provincial average.
- **Longer term (IEDM):** the median stretcher stay was about 9 h five years earlier, versus 11 h 6 min in 2023-24.

So: worse than five to ten years ago, a bad 2024-25 winter, and possibly a slight easing in 2025-26. The figures come from different periods and metrics, so treat them as indicative. I couldn't get 2026-27 year-to-date figures for Montréal.
