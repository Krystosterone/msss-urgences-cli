Here's what the indicators on the Québec dashboards mean, so you can read them yourself:
- **Occupancy rate on stretchers (taux d'occupation des civières):** the number of patients on stretchers divided by the number of staffed stretchers the ER is funded for. Over 100% means patients are on extra stretchers or in hallways. This mostly reflects patients who are already admitted or under observation. It does **not** directly tell you how long a walk-in patient will wait.
- **Number of people waiting to see a doctor (personnes en attente de prise en charge médicale):** the people in the waiting room who haven't seen a physician yet. This matters more for a walk-in patient.
- **Total people in the ER:** everyone present, on a stretcher or ambulatory.
- **Average waiting-room wait / average length of stay (DMS):** usually computed over the previous day or a recent period, not live.
- **Estimated wait times (Index Santé, Doctr):** model-based estimates, not official figures.

In both ERs, patients are seen in order of triage priority (Canadian Triage and Acuity Scale), not arrival order. A child with a minor problem can wait many hours, while a very sick child is seen quickly anywhere. For a live side-by-side comparison, open quebec.ca/SituationUrgences and filter for Montréal.
