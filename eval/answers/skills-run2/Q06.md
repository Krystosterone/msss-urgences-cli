First, a safety check: if the ankle looks deformed, the foot is cold, numb or pale, there's an open wound, or the pain is severe and you can't put any weight on it, go to the nearest ER, or call 911 if you can't get there safely. If you're unsure how serious it is, Info-Santé 811 can advise.

**I can't tell you which ER will see you fastest or how long you'll wait: MSSS's data doesn't measure that, and nobody can turn these numbers into a wait time.** ERs see patients by triage priority, not arrival order, and MSSS itself warns that a low count of patients present or waiting doesn't mean being seen by a physician sooner. The numbers don't show staffing, and they change within the hour.

**If the sprain seems minor to you, an ER may not be your fastest route at all:**

- **Info-Santé 811** (a nurse, 24/7) can tell you whether it needs to be examined or X-rayed, and where.
- **A pharmacist** can advise on caring for a minor injury.
- **Your family doctor or GMF**, or, without a family doctor, the Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP) at Québec.ca or through 811.
- **A clinic appointment** through Rendez-vous santé Québec or the clinic's own booking system.
