My reading: the Children's is running somewhat longer than usual, Sainte-Justine is in line with its norm.

**What each indicator means**

- **Stretcher occupancy** (computed by the tool from MSSS's counts) is occupied stretchers divided by functional stretchers. Over 100% is common at busy ERs: patients on overflow stretchers count as occupied but not as functional. It tells you how full the treatment area is. It doesn't measure the waiting room, and it doesn't say how fast anyone is seen.
- **Patients present** is everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or not.
- **Waiting to see a physician** ("en attente de prise en charge") counts children present who haven't been assessed by a physician yet, at every triage priority. It is not a place in line: the sickest children are seen first wherever they arrive.
- **On a stretcher 24 h / 48 h or more** counts children who have been there that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of strain on the hospital, not a measure of the wait to be seen.
- **Comparison with Montréal:** the regional mean is 152%. Sainte-Justine sits 64 points below it (Δ −64), the Children's 31 points above (Δ +31). That mean is a plain average over every reporting Montréal ER, adult ones included, so comparing the two children's ERs directly with each other is more meaningful.
- **Yesterday's stay vs the yearly norm** sets the average stay of patients who left yesterday against the same hospital's average since April 1 (fiscal 2026-2027, through period 7). It's the best "worse than usual?" signal, but it describes past patients, not how long a visit today would take.

None of these numbers predicts how long a child would wait. MSSS warns that a low count of patients present or waiting doesn't mean being seen sooner: the figures don't show staffing or triage, and they can change within the hour. If a child has worrying symptoms (very drowsy or hard to wake, trouble breathing, a seizure, a fever in a baby under 3 months), call 911 or go to the nearest ER now.
