**Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room now. A child who is very drowsy with a high fever needs to be seen without delay, so go to whichever ER is closest to you, not the one that looks less busy.**

- If your child is hard to wake, has trouble breathing, has a seizure, or you can't get there safely and quickly, call 911.
- If you're unsure what to do, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, 24/7) can advise, but don't let a call hold you back from going if your child is very drowsy.
- The nearest ER is the right one in an emergency. Montréal's two dedicated children's ERs are CHU Sainte-Justine and the Montreal Children's, but other ERs also treat children.

**Why I won't pick the "less busy" one for you:** the crowding data can't tell which ER will see your child sooner. ERs see children by how urgent their condition is, not in arrival order, so the most serious cases go first wherever they arrive. MSSS itself warns that a lower count of patients present or waiting doesn't mean being seen by a physician sooner.

For context only, not as a reason to drive farther or wait, here is what the two children's ERs reported this morning:

- **CHU Sainte-Justine:** 88% stretcher occupancy, 9 children waiting to see a physician, 33 present.
- **Montreal Children's:** 183% stretcher occupancy, 21 children waiting to see a physician, 48 present.

These are head counts at one moment, and they can change within the hour.
