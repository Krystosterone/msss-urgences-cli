A pattern needs at least about 3 snapshots per hour, ideally several weeks of them. With that caveat, here is what the first day and a half shows (stretcher occupancy, Québec time):

- **Calmest so far, in the evening and late night:**
  - Around 22h: mean 90.4% over 2 snapshots, ranging from 73.1% to 107.7%.
  - Around 20h: 100% (a single snapshot).
  - Around 23h: mean 100% over 2 snapshots, up to 115.4%.
  - Around 19h and 21h: 103.8% each (a single snapshot each).
- **Fullest so far, overnight into the morning:** around 7h and 8h it averaged 128.8%, reaching 134.6%.
- **Right now:** 135% at the 08:00 snapshot, 14 people waiting to see a physician; the Montréal mean is 152%. Against the archive's only other snapshot at this hour (123.1%), that's 11.5 points higher, close to normal, but one comparison point says very little.

So the evenings looked calmer than the mornings in these two days, which may or may not hold up. Ask again in a few weeks and the archive can give a much firmer answer, including by weekday.

Even then, it would describe past crowding, not a shorter wait: patients are seen by triage priority, and MSSS warns that a less crowded ER doesn't mean being seen sooner. Never put off care for something that could be serious to wait for a calmer hour: call 911 or go to the nearest ER. For something minor, Info-Santé 811 (24/7), a pharmacist, your own clinic or GMF, or Rendez-vous santé Québec may serve you better than any ER.
