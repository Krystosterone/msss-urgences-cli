**A 2-year-old who is very drowsy with a 39.5 °C fever needs to be seen now. Call 911, or go to the nearest emergency room right away. Don't spend time comparing ERs.** If your child is hard to wake, has trouble breathing, has a seizure, or seems to be getting worse, call 911.

- **The nearest ER is the right one.** Every ER treats children, not only the two pediatric hospitals, and ERs see the most serious cases first wherever they arrive. Crowding figures can't tell you which ER will see your child sooner, so they're not a reason to drive farther.
- If you have any doubt on the way, you can call Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, 24/7), but don't let a call delay going.

**For context only**, here is what MSSS reported at its latest hourly update for Montréal's two pediatric ERs:
- CHU Sainte-Justine: 88% stretcher occupancy, 9 children waiting to see a physician.
- Montreal Children's: 183% stretcher occupancy, 21 children waiting to see a physician.

These are head counts at one moment, not wait times. MSSS itself warns that a lower count doesn't mean being seen sooner: the numbers don't show staffing or how urgent each child is, and they change quickly. A drowsy child with a high fever is assessed by how serious their condition is, not by how many people are in the waiting room.
