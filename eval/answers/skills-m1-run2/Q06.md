**The data can't tell you which ER would see you fastest or how long you'd wait, so I can't pick one for you. For the wait, Québec.ca publishes an official estimate for each ER. And for a sprain, an ER may not be your only option.**

**How long you'd wait.** MSSS's open data has no wait times. Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows, for each ER, an estimated wait before seeing a physician for a *non-priority* case, from MSSS's provincial ER console. It depends on the staff and the patients present when you arrive, it doesn't apply to urgent cases, and it isn't a promise.

**Other routes for a sprain**
- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse, 24/7, can tell you whether the ankle needs to be seen and where.
- **A pharmacist:** can advise on minor problems and, for some conditions, prescribe.
- **Your family doctor or GMF**, if you have one. Without one, the Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP), online at Québec.ca or through 811.
- **Rendez-vous santé Québec** or a clinic's own booking system, to find an appointment.

**When it's more than a sprain:** go to an ER if you can't put any weight on the foot, the ankle or foot looks deformed, it's numb, cold or pale, there's an open wound, or the pain and swelling are severe or getting worse.

- **Stretcher occupancy** is how full the stretcher area is (it can top 100% because overflow stretchers count as occupied). **People waiting** counts everyone present who hasn't seen a physician yet, at every triage priority. The two often point in different directions: Lachine is at 157% with 4 people waiting, while the CHUM is at 145% with 42 waiting. Neither tells you how fast you'd be seen: ERs see the most urgent cases first, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
