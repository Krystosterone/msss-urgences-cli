**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is much more crowded than Sainte-Justine's: its stretcher area is over capacity, and more children are waiting to see a physician.**

| | CHU Sainte-Justine | Montreal Children's |
|---|---|---|
| Stretcher occupancy | 88% (14 occupied / 16 functional) | 183% (22 occupied / 12 functional) |
| Waiting to see a physician | 9 | 21 |
| Total in the ER | 33 | 48 |
| On a stretcher over 24 h / over 48 h | 0 / 0 | 3 / 1 |
| Yesterday's mean stretcher stay | 6.6 h (this year: 6.4 h, Δ +2%) | 10.3 h (this year: 8.8 h, Δ +16%) |
| Yesterday's mean walk-in stay | 4.3 h (this year: 4.1 h, Δ +3%) | 5.2 h (this year: 4.5 h, Δ +16%) |

For context, Montréal's rate is 161% (MSSS's regional total, adult ERs included), and the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152%.

**What the indicators mean**
- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers. It shows how full the ER's stretcher area is. It goes over 100% because MSSS counts overflow stretchers (in hallways, for example) as occupied but not as functional. Higher means more crowded. It doesn't measure the waiting room or how fast anyone is seen.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't yet been assessed by a doctor, at every triage priority. It isn't a place in line: the sickest children are seen first.
- **Total in the ER** counts every patient present, on a stretcher or in the waiting area.
- **On a stretcher over 24 h / 48 h** counts children who have been on an ER stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of strain on the hospital as a whole.
- **Yesterday's mean stay** is the average time between arrival and departure for patients who *left yesterday*. The comparison is with the same hospital's average since April 1 (fiscal year 2026-2027, through period 7). Sainte-Justine's +2% is normal day-to-day variation. The Children's +16% on both measures is somewhat longer than usual for it.
- **The Montréal rate and mean** mix adult and pediatric ERs, so comparing the two pediatric ERs directly says more than comparing either with the regional figure (Sainte-Justine Δ -64 points from the mean, the Children's Δ +31).

**What they can't tell you**
- None of this predicts how long a child would wait. Children are seen by how urgent their condition is, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner; the numbers don't show staffing and can change within the hour. For an estimated wait for a non-priority case, Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) gives one per ER. It's an estimate, not a promise, and doesn't apply to urgent cases.
- For advice on whether to come in at all, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7. If a child's symptoms seem serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
