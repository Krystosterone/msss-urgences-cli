**Not steadily. On the yearly indicators MSSS publishes, strain on Montréal's ERs peaked in 2022-2023 and eased over the next three years; by 2025-2026 it was close to its 2021-2022 level. Whether this year is worse can't be said fairly yet, because this year's figures cover fewer ERs.**

**What I used.** MSSS's cumulative ER file has no stretcher-occupancy rate, so I used the strain indicators it does have: the mean time patients spent on an ER stretcher, the share of stretcher visits that lasted 24 h or 48 h or more, and the mean delay from triage to the first physician. Lower is better for all of them. Each year is cumulated over the same window, April 1 to the end of period 7 (roughly mid-October).

| Fiscal year (April to period 7) | Montréal ERs in the data | Mean stretcher stay | Stretcher visits ≥ 24 h | ≥ 48 h | Mean delay to physician | Visits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 21 | 18.0 h | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2.6 h | 449,894 |
| 2022-2023 | 21 | 19.9 h | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2.8 h | 470,449 |
| 2023-2024 | 21 | 19.2 h | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2.7 h | 458,360 |
| 2024-2025 | 21 | 18.7 h | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2.8 h | 506,551 |
| 2025-2026 | 21 | 18.4 h | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2.6 h | 489,669 |
| 2026-2027 | 17 | 19.0 h | 23.9% | 7.3% | 2.7 h | 425,586 |

**Reading it**
- **2021-2022 to 2025-2026** (five years, the same Montréal ERs in each): every strain indicator rose into 2022-2023. Stretcher stays and the long-stay shares then fell each year, while the delay to see a physician went up and down and ended close to where it started. In 2025-2026, the share of stretcher visits over 24 h was below its 2021-2022 level, the share over 48 h was the same, and the mean stretcher stay was a little longer. Visits (volume, not quality) rose over the period, peaking in 2024-2025.
- **2026-2027 isn't comparable:** the file has 17 Montréal ERs this year against 21 in every earlier year (Δ -4). In the live data, the ERs with no 2026-2027 row are Hôpital du Sacré-Coeur-de-Montréal, Hôpital Fleury, Hôpital Jean-Talon and Hôpital en santé mentale Albert-Prévost, most likely the same set. Their absence changes both the totals and the averages, so neither this year's lower visit count nor its changes in stays and shares can be read as a real improvement or deterioration.
- **Occupancy over time:** the hourly occupancy archive only starts on 2026-11-03T22:45, so it can't show a trend. Right now Montréal's rate is 161%, against 130% for Québec as a whole, but one snapshot says nothing about a trend.
- These are averages over months of past visits. They describe how the system has performed, not how long a visit today would take.

_Source: MSSS cumulative ER file (Fichier cumulatif des données des urgences), fiscal years 2021-2022 to 2026-2027, each April 1 to period 7. Live rate: MSSS hourly ER file, published 2026-11-05T08:45._
