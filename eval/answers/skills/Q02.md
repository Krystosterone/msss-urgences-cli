**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is more crowded than Sainte-Justine's on every stretcher measure, while Sainte-Justine has more children waiting to see a physician.**

- Sainte-Justine: 125% stretcher occupancy, 39 children waiting to see a physician, 92 present in all, none on a stretcher for over 24 h.
- Montreal Children's: 175% occupancy, 27 waiting, 83 present, 4 on a stretcher for over 24 h (1 over 48 h).
- Yesterday's average stretcher stay: 8.3 h at Sainte-Justine and 13.4 h at the Children's, against fiscal-year norms of 6.4 h and 8.8 h.

What the indicators mean:

- **Stretcher occupancy** is patients on stretchers divided by the ER's functional stretchers. MSSS counts overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional, so it often exceeds 100%. It measures how full the stretcher area is, not the waiting room.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts everyone present who hasn't seen a physician yet, at every priority. It isn't a place in line: children are seen by triage priority, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
- **Over 24 h / 48 h** counts children on a stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs: a sign of strain on the hospital.
- **Average stay** is for the patients who left yesterday, from arrival to departure. It's a past average, not anyone's expected visit.
