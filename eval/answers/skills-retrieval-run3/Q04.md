**For non-urgent ear pain you have several routes besides the ER, and 15 clinics in the Ahuntsic – Montréal-Nord local network have recently offered appointments through the provincial booking hub.**

**Ways to get seen**

- **A pharmacist** can advise you on ear pain and, for some conditions, prescribe.
- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse's advice by phone, 24/7, including whether you need to see someone and how soon.
- **Your own family doctor or GMF**, if you have one.
- **No family doctor:** the Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP), online at Québec.ca or through 811. Check Québec.ca for current eligibility.
- **To book an actual slot:** Rendez-vous santé Québec, or the clinic's own booking system.

**Which nearby clinics usually offer appointments** (MSSS clinic file, RLS d'Ahuntsic - Montréal-Nord, 2026-10-22 to 2026-10-28, 7 of 7 days with data)

- 15 clinics offered at least one appointment that week, most of them on weekdays.
- On Thursday 2026-10-22 (the same weekday as today), District Médical offered 342 physician appointments, District Médical Junior 176, Centre médical Mieux-Être (succ. Ahuntsic) 150, and Clinique médicale Omnisanté 95.
- Some clinics also offer nurse appointments (nurses and specialized nurse practitioners): Clinique médicale Désy offered 56 on 2026-10-22, CLSC Ahuntsic 38 on 2026-10-28.
- Evening physician appointments (scheduled 18h–21h): District Médical offered some every day of the week, 25 on 2026-10-28; Clinique médicale Désy offered 11 and CLSC de Montréal-Nord 5 that day.
- Weekends are thinner: on Sunday 2026-10-25, District Médical offered 56 physician appointments.

**What this can't tell you:** these are appointments clinics *offered* a week or more ago, not openings today. Some were booked, some are reserved for the clinic's own patients, and clinics outside the booking hub aren't counted. More offers doesn't mean better odds for you. The file is published weekly (Thursdays at 14h), so a newer week is due this afternoon.

**When not to wait for a clinic:** a high fever, swelling or redness behind the ear, a stiff neck or severe headache, confusion, or weakness on one side of the face. Then call 811, or go to the ER or call 911 if it seems serious.
