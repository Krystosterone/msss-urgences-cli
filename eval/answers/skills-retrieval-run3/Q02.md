**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is much more crowded than Sainte-Justine's.**

- **CHU Sainte-Justine:** 88% stretcher occupancy (14 of 16 stretchers occupied), 9 children waiting to see a physician, 33 present in total, none on a stretcher for 24 h or more.
- **Montreal Children's (L'Hôpital de Montréal pour enfants):** 183% occupancy (22 occupied for 12 functional stretchers), 21 waiting to see a physician, 48 present, 3 on a stretcher for over 24 h, including 1 for over 48 h.
- **Against their own norm:** at the Children's, patients who left yesterday stayed 10.3 h on a stretcher, +16% over its 8.8 h mean so far this fiscal year: somewhat longer than usual. At Sainte-Justine it was +2%, in line with its norm.

**What the indicators mean**

- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied ÷ functional stretchers. Going over 100% is common at busy ERs, because MSSS counts overflow stretchers as occupied. Higher means a more crowded stretcher area. It doesn't measure the waiting room or how fast a child is seen.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't seen a doctor yet, at every triage priority. It isn't a place in line: the sickest children are seen first, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
- **Present** is everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or in the waiting room.
- **On a stretcher over 24 h / 48 h** counts children who have been there that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs: a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Compared with Montréal:** the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152% (Sainte-Justine Δ -64, the Children's Δ 31), and Montréal's rate is 161%. That mean mixes adult and pediatric ERs, so comparing the two pediatric ERs directly is more meaningful.
- **Yesterday's stay vs the norm** compares the mean stretcher stay of patients who left yesterday with the same ER's mean since April 1 (fiscal 2026-2027, through period 7). It's past data, not the stay of a child arriving now.

None of these is a wait time. For an estimated wait for a non-priority case, see Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences): an estimate that depends on the staff and patients present when you arrive, that doesn't apply to urgent cases, and isn't a promise. If your child seems seriously ill, call 911 or go to the nearest ER; if you're unsure, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7.
