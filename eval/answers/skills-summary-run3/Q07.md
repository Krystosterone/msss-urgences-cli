**Not steadily. By MSSS's fiscal-year strain indicators, Montréal's ERs were most strained in 2022-2023, then eased, and by 2025-2026 were back to roughly their 2021-2022 levels. This year's figures can't yet be compared with earlier years, because they cover fewer ERs.**

The open data has no occupancy rate over years, so this uses the strain indicators in MSSS's cumulative ER file, for the Montréal region, each year from April 1 to period 7 (roughly mid-October), the same window every year:

| Fiscal year | ERs in the data | Mean stretcher stay | Stretcher stays 24 h+ | Stretcher stays 48 h+ | Mean delay to first physician |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 21 | 18.0 h | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2022-2023 | 21 | 19.9 h | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2023-2024 | 21 | 19.2 h | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2.7 h |
| 2024-2025 | 21 | 18.7 h | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2025-2026 | 21 | 18.4 h | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2026-2027 (not comparable) | 17 | 19.0 h | 23.9% | 7.3% | 2.7 h |

What that shows, over the five comparable years (2021-2022 to 2025-2026, 21 ERs each year):

- **The peak was 2022-2023:** the longest stretcher stays and the highest shares of stays over 24 h and over 48 h.
- **Between that peak and 2025-2026, every indicator eased.** In 2025-2026, the share of stretcher stays over 24 h was a little lower than in 2021-2022, the 48 h share was the same, and the average delay to see a physician was about the same; only the mean stretcher stay was slightly longer (by under half an hour).
- **So "worsened over time" doesn't fit these years:** it got worse, then better. These are averages over months of visits, at all triage priorities; they describe how the ERs performed, not how long a visit today would take.

**Why 2026-2027 is set apart:** this year's Montréal total covers 17 ERs, against 21 last year, so its averages describe a different set of hospitals and aren't comparable. The live ER file finds no 2026-2027 row for four Montréal ERs (Sacré-Cœur, Fleury, Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost), which is probably, though not certainly, the same four.

**Right now** Montréal's stretcher occupancy is 161% (MSSS's regional rate, 08:45 update), but the archived occupancy history only goes back to 2026-11-03, far too short to call a trend.

_Sources: MSSS cumulative ER file ("Fichier cumulatif des données des urgences"), fiscal years 2021-2022 to 2026-2027, each April 1 → period 7; MSSS hourly ER file published 2026-11-05 at 08:45._
