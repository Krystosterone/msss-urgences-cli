**A 2-year-old with a fever who is very drowsy needs to be seen now: call 911, or go to the nearest emergency room right away. Don't pick a hospital by how busy it looks.**

- A child who is very drowsy or hard to wake is one of the warning signs that call for emergency care. The same goes for trouble breathing or a seizure.
- Any ER can assess a child; the nearest one is the right one in an emergency. If you're unsure what to do on the way, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, 24/7) can advise you, but don't let a call delay you.
- ERs see children by how urgent their condition is, not by arrival order or by how full the ER is. A drowsy child with a high fever will be triaged accordingly wherever you arrive, so a "less busy" ER won't necessarily mean being seen faster, and the open data can't predict who is seen sooner.

For context only, not as a reason to drive farther: at the 08:45 update on 2026-11-05, Montréal's two pediatric ERs reported:

- CHU Sainte-Justine: 88% stretcher occupancy, 9 children waiting to see a physician.
- Montreal Children's (Glen site): 183% stretcher occupancy, 21 children waiting to see a physician.

These are head counts at one moment, not wait times; MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner, and the numbers change quickly.
