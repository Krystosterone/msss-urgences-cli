| On a stretcher over 24 h | 0 | 3 |
| On a stretcher over 48 h | 0 | 1 |

**What each indicator means**

- **Stretcher occupancy** is occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers (computed by the CLI from MSSS's counts). Over 100% is common at busy ERs, because MSSS counts patients on overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional. Higher means a fuller stretcher area; it says nothing about how fast a child will be seen.
- **Children present** is everyone registered in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or in the waiting room.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't been assessed by a doctor yet, at every triage priority. It isn't a place in line: the sickest children are seen first.
- **Over 24 h / over 48 h** count children who have been on a stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of hospital strain, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Compared with Montréal:** Montréal's rate is 161% (MSSS's regional total) and the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152%. Sainte-Justine is 64 points below that mean and the Children's 31 points above, but the mean mixes adult and pediatric ERs, so comparing the two children's ERs directly is more meaningful.
- **Against their own norm** (yesterday's mean stretcher stay vs fiscal year 2026-2027 to date, April 1 → period 7): Sainte-Justine 6.6 h vs 6.4 h (+2%, normal); the Children's 10.3 h vs 8.8 h (+16%, somewhat longer than usual). These are past stays, not the stay of a child arriving now.

**What this can't tell you:** none of these numbers is a wait time, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner (staffing and triage aren't in the data). For an official wait estimate for non-urgent cases, Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows one per ER; it depends on the staff and patients present when you arrive, doesn't apply to urgent cases and isn't a promise. For advice on whether a child needs the ER at all, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7; for serious symptoms, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
