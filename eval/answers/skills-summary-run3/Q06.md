**The open data can't tell you which ER will see you fastest, and it has no wait times, so I can't give you either. For an estimate, Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows, for each ER, an estimated wait before seeing a physician for a non-priority case.** That estimate depends on the staff and patients present when you arrive, doesn't apply to urgent cases, and isn't a promise.

For a sprain you describe as minor, an ER isn't your only route:

- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse can advise you by phone, 24/7, including whether the ankle needs to be seen and where.
- **A pharmacist** can advise on minor problems and, for some conditions, prescribe.
- **Your family doctor or GMF**, or without one the Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP) at Québec.ca or through 811; clinic slots can be booked through Rendez-vous santé Québec or the clinic itself.
- Go to an ER if you can't put any weight on the foot, the ankle looks deformed, the foot is numb, cold or pale, or the pain and swelling are severe.

How to read these: occupancy is how full the stretcher area is, and "waiting" counts everyone present who hasn't seen a physician yet, at every priority. ERs see people by triage priority, so a sprain waits behind more serious cases wherever you go, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner: staffing isn't in the data, and the numbers change quickly.
