**Over five years, overcrowding has gone up and down rather than steadily getting worse. It's somewhat worse this year than last, but not at the 2022–23 peak.** Montréal is also consistently the most congested region in Québec.

**Same period each year (April 1 to end of financial period 7, all Montréal ERs; MSSS cumulative file):**

| Fiscal year | Avg. stay, stretcher patients | Avg. stay, walk-in (ambulatory) | Avg. stay, all visits | Stretcher patients > 48 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-22 | 18.0 h | 5.6 h | 10.2 h | 11,140 |
| 2022-23 | **19.9 h** | **5.9 h** | 11.0 h | **15,730** |
| 2023-24 | 19.2 h | 5.7 h | 10.9 h | 13,637 |
| 2024-25 | 18.7 h | 5.7 h | 10.7 h | 14,021 |
| 2025-26 | 18.4 h | 5.3 h | 10.5 h | 12,880 |
| 2026-27 | 19.0 h | 5.6 h | **11.0 h** | 12,481 |

How to read it:
- From 2025-26 to 2026-27, average stretcher stays rose about 3% (18.4 → 19.0 h) and walk-in stays about 5% (5.3 → 5.6 h). So this year is worse than last year.
- It's still below the 2022-23 peak on stretcher stays and on > 48 h patients.
- Compared with 2021-22, stays are slightly longer.

**Stretcher occupancy:**
- Recent days: Index Santé's 30-day series for Montréal averages **148%**. Daily values ranged from 127% to 166% since early October.
- This morning: **161%** (763 occupied / 473 functional stretchers at 08:45), versus 130% for Québec as a whole.
- In early 2025, Santé Québec's bulletins reported Montréal occupancy rising from about 126–127% the year before to about 140–141%.

I didn't find an official multi-year occupancy-rate series. The longer-term picture above is based on stay lengths.

Caveats:
- Year-to-date figures cover only April to mid-autumn, before the winter respiratory peak.
- Average stays are influenced by how many admitted patients are waiting for a bed. That is a hospital-capacity problem as much as an ER problem.
