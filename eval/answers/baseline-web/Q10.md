**Yes, Maisonneuve-Rosemont (HMR) looks worse than its own norm this year, mainly in how long people are staying.** Its occupancy is high, but HMR is often that high.

**Today** (MSSS hourly data, Thu Nov 5, published 08:45):
- Stretcher occupancy **133%** (72 patients on 54 functional stretchers).
- 99 people in the ER, **30 waiting to see a doctor**.
- **31 patients on a stretcher > 24 h**, 8 of them > 48 h.
- Québec.ca's estimated wait for a non-priority case: **~10 h 28**, one of the longest in Montréal.

**Average stays: yesterday vs this fiscal year so far** (April 1 to period 7, 2026-27, MSSS cumulative file):

| Measure | Yesterday | 2026-27 year to date | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avg. stay on a stretcher | **29 h 25** | 22 h 04 | ~+7 h (+33%) |
| Avg. stay, walk-in (ambulatory) | **7 h 47** | 5 h 47 | ~+2 h (+34%) |

The year-to-date average time to first see a doctor was already 4.4 h, the longest of the six years in the file (3.2–3.8 h in 2021-22 to 2025-26).

**Occupancy compared with HMR's usual level:**
- The MSSS cumulative file doesn't include occupancy rates.
- Doctr's typical profile for HMR is about 117% at 9 am, and about 126% on an average Thursday.
- So 133% is moderately above HMR's usual level, not extreme. HMR's normal is already over capacity.

**Bottom line:** today is a worse-than-usual day at HMR. Stays are about a third longer than this year's average, and the estimated wait for a minor problem is over 10 hours.
- If your problem isn't urgent, a clinic or Info-Santé 811 is a better route.
- If it is urgent, go anyway: triage will prioritize you.

Caveats:
- The "yesterday" averages are calculated daily. The other figures are a snapshot from 8 am.
- The year-to-date figures run only to mid-autumn and don't include winter.
