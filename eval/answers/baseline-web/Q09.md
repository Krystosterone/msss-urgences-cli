**Usually overnight, and on Sundays and Mondays.** Mid-afternoon and mid-week are usually the most crowded.

Doctr, a third-party site, builds averages of Hôpital de Verdun's stretcher occupancy from the hourly MSSS data:

**By hour of day (typical):**
- **Lowest: midnight to about 4 am (≈ 88–92%).**
- Rises steadily from about 6 am (97%), passes 100% around 7–8 am, and peaks around **2–5 pm (≈ 114–115%)**.
- Eases in the evening: about 104% at 8 pm and 96% at 10 pm.

**By day of week (typical daily average):**
- **Monday ≈ 86% and Sunday ≈ 87% are the quietest.**
- Tuesday ≈ 103%, Friday ≈ 108%, Saturday ≈ 104%.
- **Wednesday ≈ 117% and Thursday ≈ 115% are the busiest.**

**Right now** (Thursday 8:00–8:45 am), Verdun is busier than usual:
- 135% occupancy, all 26 stretchers full plus extras (35 patients on stretchers).
- 49 people in the ER, 14 waiting to see a doctor.
- Québec.ca's estimated non-priority wait: about **4 h 35**.
- Yesterday's average waiting-room stay: 6 h 48.

Caveats:
- Doctr doesn't say what period its averages cover.
- Stretcher occupancy mostly reflects admitted and stretcher patients. It doesn't map exactly onto how long a walk-in patient waits.
- MSSS doesn't publish an official hour-by-hour history; its hourly file is overwritten every hour.
- Treat this as a rough pattern. If you're unwell, don't wait for a quiet hour.
