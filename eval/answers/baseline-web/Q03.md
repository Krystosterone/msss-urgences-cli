**Please don't choose the ER by how busy it is. A 2-year-old with 39.5 °C who is *very drowsy* needs to be seen now.** Go to the nearest children's ER, or the nearest ER of any kind, right away. Unusual drowsiness or lethargy with a fever is a warning sign.

**Call 911** instead of driving if your child:
- is hard to wake or doesn't respond normally
- is breathing with difficulty
- has a purple or red rash that doesn't fade when pressed
- has a stiff neck or a seizure
- looks blue or grey
- isn't drinking, or has barely peed

Tell the triage nurse right away that your child is "very drowsy". Triage ranks children by severity, not by arrival time. A drowsy, feverish toddler is usually seen quickly even when the waiting room is full. The posted wait times are for **non-priority** cases and won't apply to your child.

For your information, the 8:00–8:45 am readings:
- **CHU Sainte-Justine:** 88% stretcher occupancy, 9 children waiting to see a doctor, estimated non-priority wait ~1 h 49.
- **Montreal Children's Hospital (Glen):** 183% occupancy, 21 waiting, estimated non-priority wait ~2 h 50.

If both are equally close, Sainte-Justine is currently less congested. But the right choice is whichever you can reach fastest.

If you're unsure, Info-Santé **811** (option 1, 24/7) can advise you while you get ready. With your child very drowsy, don't wait for a call-back.
