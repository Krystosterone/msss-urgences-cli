The MCH is also well above its usual level for this hour. Doctr's typical profile puts it at about 102% around 9 am, so 183% is high. Sainte-Justine is only slightly above its typical ~80%.

**What the indicators mean:**
- **Stretcher occupancy (taux d'occupation des civières):** occupied stretchers divided by functional (staffed) stretchers. Above 100% means patients are on extra stretchers in hallways, usually because they're waiting for a bed upstairs. It measures congestion for the sickest patients, not how long a walk-in waits.
- **Total people in the ER:** everyone registered, both stretcher patients and walking ("ambulatory") patients.
- **Waiting to see a doctor (en attente de prise en charge):** people triaged but not yet seen by a physician. This is the closest live clue to the queue in the waiting room.
- **> 24 h / > 48 h on a stretcher:** long stays, mostly people waiting for admission. They show the hospital is backed up.
- **Average stay (DMS):** the average time spent in the waiting room or on a stretcher. It is calculated for **yesterday**, not right now.
- **Estimated wait (Québec.ca):** a model estimate for a non-priority case before seeing a doctor. It depends on staffing and on how sick the other patients are.

All of this is a snapshot and can change within the hour. Triage always sees the most urgent children first. If your child is sick, go to the ER that is closest or most practical. Don't drive across town to chase a lower number.
