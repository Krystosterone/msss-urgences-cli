**Not over the last few years, by the strain measures MSSS publishes: Montréal's ER indicators got worse in 2022-2023, then eased, and by 2025-2026 were back near their 2021-2022 level. The current year can't be compared fairly yet, because four Montréal ERs are missing from its data.**

The cumulative ER file has no occupancy rate, so this uses its strain indicators for Montréal's ERs as a whole, each year counted from April 1 to the end of period 7 (about mid-October), so every year covers the same window:

| Fiscal year | Mean stay on a stretcher | Stretcher stays over 24 h | over 48 h | Mean delay to see a physician |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 18 h 02 | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2 h 34 |
| 2022-2023 | 19 h 53 | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2 h 50 |
| 2023-2024 | 19 h 13 | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2 h 41 |
| 2024-2025 | 18 h 43 | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2 h 45 |
| 2025-2026 | 18 h 26 | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2 h 35 |

- Those five years each cover 21 ERs, so they compare like with like. Lower is better on every column. Over five years, the stretcher stay and the share of 24 h+ stays peaked in 2022-2023 and have come down each year since; the mean delay to see a physician also peaked in 2022-2023 and in 2025-2026 was close to its 2021-2022 level. The 2025-2026 stretcher stay is still somewhat longer than in 2021-2022, while its share of 24 h+ stays is lower.
- Volume went up: 449,894 visits in 2021-2022 against 489,669 in 2025-2026 (same window).
- **2026-2027 so far is not comparable:** its data covers 17 ERs instead of 21. The four Montréal ERs with no row this year are Hôpital du Sacré-Coeur-de-Montréal, Hôpital Fleury, Hôpital Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost, which are also not reporting in the hourly file right now. For the 17 that are there: a stretcher stay of about 19 h 00, 23.9% of stretcher stays over 24 h and 7.3% over 48 h. Because the set of ERs changed, a rise or fall against last year can't be read as a trend.
- **Right now** Montréal's stretcher occupancy is 161% (MSSS's regional total), with 272 people on a stretcher for over 24 h. That's a single snapshot, not a trend, and the archived hourly history only goes back to 2026-11-03, far too short to say anything about years.

These are averages over months of past visits; they describe how the region's ERs have been doing, not how long a visit today would take.

_Sources: MSSS cumulative ER file (fiscal years 2021-2022 to 2026-2027, each cumulated April 1 → period 7); MSSS hourly ER file, published 2026-11-05 08:45._
