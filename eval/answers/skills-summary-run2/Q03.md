**Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room now.** A child with a high fever who is very drowsy needs to be seen urgently. Go to whichever ER is closest, or call 911 if your child is hard to wake, has trouble breathing, has a seizure or seems to be getting worse. Don't drive farther or wait because one ER looks less busy. If you are unsure, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, 24/7) can advise you, but don't let the call hold you up.

Why crowding shouldn't decide this: ERs see children by how urgent their condition is, not in arrival order, so a very drowsy child is assessed as a priority wherever you arrive. The open data can't tell which ER would see your child sooner, and any ER can assess and treat a child, not only the pediatric ones.

Since you asked, here is what MSSS reports for Montréal's two pediatric ERs right now, as background only:

- **CHU Sainte-Justine:** 88% stretcher occupancy, 9 children waiting to see a physician.
- **Montreal Children's (Glen site):** 183% stretcher occupancy, 21 children waiting to see a physician.

These are head counts at one moment, not wait times, and MSSS warns that a lower count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
