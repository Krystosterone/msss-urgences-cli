**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is much more crowded than Sainte-Justine's.**

- **CHU Sainte-Justine:** stretcher occupancy 88% (14 occupied of 16 functional stretchers), 9 children waiting to see a physician, 33 children present, 0 on a stretcher for over 24 h and 0 for over 48 h.
- **Montreal Children's (Glen site):** stretcher occupancy 183% (22 occupied of 12 functional), 21 children waiting to see a physician, 48 present, 3 on a stretcher for over 24 h, 1 of them for over 48 h.
- **Against their own norms this fiscal year:** children who left Sainte-Justine's ER yesterday spent 6 h 35 on a stretcher on average, +2% against its 2026-2027 average so far (normal). At the Children's it was 10 h 16, +16% against its average of about 8 h 50 (somewhat longer than usual).
- For context, Montréal's rate is 161% (all its ERs, adult ones included).

**What the indicators mean**

- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers. It shows how full the stretcher area is. Over 100% is common at busy ERs: MSSS counts patients on extra, overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional. It says nothing about the waiting room or how fast a child is seen.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't been assessed by a physician yet, at every triage priority. It isn't a place in line: the sickest children are seen first.
- **Present** is everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or not.
- **On a stretcher over 24 h / 48 h** counts children who have been on a stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Mean stretcher stay** is the average time from arrival to departure for stretcher patients who left *yesterday*, compared here with the same hospital's mean since April 1 (fiscal 2026-2027, through period 7). It's a past average, not anyone's expected visit.
- **Δ from the Montréal mean** (Sainte-Justine Δ −64, Children's Δ +31) compares each with the mean of Montréal's ERs, which mixes adult and pediatric ERs; comparing the two pediatric ERs directly is more telling.

None of these is a wait time. MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean a child will be seen sooner: the numbers don't show staffing or triage, and they change quickly. If you're deciding whether to go at all, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7; if your child's symptoms could be serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
