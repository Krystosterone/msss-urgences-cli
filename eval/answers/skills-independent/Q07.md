**Not steadily. Montréal's ER strain indicators got worse into 2022-2023, then eased back to about their 2021-2022 level by 2025-2026. This year's figures can't be compared directly, because fewer hospitals are in them.**

Montréal region, each fiscal year from April 1 to the end of period 7 (the same window every year), lower is better on every column:

| Fiscal year | Hospitals in the data | Mean stretcher stay | Stretcher stays over 24 h | Over 48 h | Mean delay to first physician |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 21 | 18.0 h | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2022-2023 | 21 | 19.9 h | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2023-2024 | 21 | 19.2 h | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2.7 h |
| 2024-2025 | 21 | 18.7 h | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2025-2026 | 21 | 18.4 h | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2026-2027 | 17 | 19.0 h | 23.9% | 7.3% | 2.7 h |

- **Five comparable years (2021-2022 to 2025-2026, 21 hospitals each year):** every indicator peaked in 2022-2023 and came back down afterwards, though not in a straight line. By 2025-2026, the share of stretcher stays over 24 h (24.6%) was below its 2021-2022 level (25.2%), the over-48 h share was back at 6.7%, and the mean stretcher stay was slightly longer (18.4 h against 18.0 h). My reading: a bad patch around 2022-2023, not a lasting worsening.
- **2026-2027 so far is not comparable.** The region's total covers 17 hospitals instead of 21 (4 fewer), so its figures describe a different set of ERs, not a change over time. Four Montréal hospitals have no validated 2026-2027 data in the file yet: Hôpital du Sacré-Coeur-de-Montréal, Hôpital Fleury, Hôpital Jean-Talon and Hôpital en santé mentale Albert-Prévost. Their absence says nothing about whether they improved or worsened.
- These are averages over months of past visits. They describe how Montréal's ERs have performed, not how long a visit today would take. Tonight's snapshot (a Montréal mean of 144% stretcher occupancy, published 2026-11-04 21:45) is a single moment and can't show a trend either.
