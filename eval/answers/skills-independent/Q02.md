**What each indicator means**

- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers. Going over 100% is normal at busy ERs, because MSSS counts overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional. Higher means a more crowded stretcher area. It doesn't measure the waiting room or how fast a child is seen.
- **Patients present** counts everyone registered in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or not.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't had a first physician assessment yet, at every triage priority. It is not a place in line: the sickest children are seen first.
- **Over 24 h / 48 h** counts children who have been on a stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It signals strain on the hospital, not the wait to be seen.
- **Versus the Montréal mean** is occupancy minus the plain average of all reporting Montréal ERs, adult ones included, so comparing the two pediatric ERs with each other is more meaningful than comparing either with the regional mean.
- **Yesterday's mean stretcher stay** is the average time from arrival to departure for stretcher patients who left yesterday, set against the same hospital's average since April 1 (fiscal year 2026-2027, through period 7). My reading: both ERs ran noticeably longer than their own norm yesterday, the Children's more so. It is a past average, not a forecast.

**What the numbers can't tell you:** these are head counts at one moment. They don't show staffing or triage, they change quickly, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner, so they can't tell which ER would see a child faster. For advice on whether to come in at all, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse) answers at any hour; if a child's symptoms could be serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
