Compared with its own norm (fiscal 2026-2027, April 1 → period 7):

- **Stretcher patients** who left yesterday stayed 29.4 h on average, 33% more than its 22.1 h so far this year.
- **Walk-in (ambulatory) patients** who left yesterday stayed 7.8 h on average, 35% more than its 5.8 h so far this year.
- My reading: both are past the +25% mark I treat as "noticeably longer than usual", so yesterday was a clearly heavier day than its norm. These are *yesterday's* stays, not the stay of someone arriving now.

Right now (MSSS snapshot taken 2026-11-05 at 08:00, published at 08:45):

- 133% stretcher occupancy (72 occupied for 54 functional stretchers), 99 patients present, 30 waiting to see a physician, 31 on a stretcher for 24 h or more (8 of them for 48 h or more).
- That's below MSSS's Montréal rate of 161% and below the mean of Montréal's ERs, 152% (Δ -19). So: very busy in absolute terms, but not more crowded than the region this morning.
- The hour-by-hour history can't say whether this morning is unusual for it: the archive only starts 2026-11-03 at 22:45, so its 8h "typical" value rests on a single other snapshot.

Context on its year (not about today): so far in 2026-2027, 36.6% of its stretcher visits lasted 24 h or more, against 28.5% at the same point in 2025-2026, and its mean stretcher stay was 22.1 h against 18.6 h. So even its "norm" this year is heavier than last year's. Its average delay from triage to a first physician this year is 4.4 h; that's an average over all patients and priorities, not anyone's expected wait.

None of these numbers is a wait time: patients are seen by triage priority, and MSSS warns that the counts don't predict how soon someone is seen.
