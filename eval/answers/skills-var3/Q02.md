| Yesterday's mean stretcher stay vs this fiscal year | 6.6 h vs 6.4 h (+2%) | 10.3 h vs 8.8 h (+16%) |
| Yesterday's mean walk-in stay vs this fiscal year | 4.3 h vs 4.1 h (+3%) | 5.2 h vs 4.5 h (+16%) |

For context, MSSS's Montréal rate is 161%, and the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152%; both mix adult and pediatric ERs, so comparing the two children's ERs with each other says more.

My reading: Sainte-Justine is having a normal day for it (its stays are within ~10% of its norm), while the Children's is both fuller right now and running somewhat longer stays than usual this year.

**What each indicator means**

- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers (computed from MSSS's counts). It shows how full the stretcher area is, not the waiting room. Above 100% is common: MSSS counts patients on overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional, which is how the Children's reaches 183%.
- **Patients present** = everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or not.
- **Waiting to see a physician** = children present who haven't been assessed by a physician yet, at every triage priority. It isn't a place in line: the sickest children are seen first.
- **On a stretcher 24 h+ / 48 h+** = children who have been on a stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Mean stay (stretcher / walk-in)** = average time from arrival to departure for patients who *left yesterday*, set against the hospital's own average since April 1 (fiscal 2026-2027, through period 7). Roughly ±10% is ordinary day-to-day noise; +10% to +25% is "somewhat longer than usual".

**What they can't tell you:** none of these is a wait time. MSSS warns that a low number present or waiting doesn't mean a child will be seen sooner: the counts don't show staffing or triage, and they change quickly. For an estimate, Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows each ER's estimated wait for a non-priority case; it depends on who is there when you arrive, doesn't apply to urgent cases and isn't a promise. If your child is unwell and you're unsure whether to come in, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7; if symptoms could be serious (very drowsy, trouble breathing, a seizure), call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
