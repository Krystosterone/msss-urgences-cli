**The data can't tell you which ER would see you fastest, or how long you'd wait: MSSS's ER data contains no wait times, and patients are seen by triage priority, not in arrival order. For an actual estimate, check Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences).** That page shows, for each ER, an estimated wait before seeing a physician for a *non-priority* case. It depends on the staff and the patients present when you arrive, doesn't apply to urgent cases, and isn't a promise.

**For a sprain you may not need the ER at all.** Options MSSS recommends for minor problems:

- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse can advise you by phone, 24/7, on whether and where to be seen.
- **A pharmacist:** can advise on minor problems and, for some conditions, prescribe.
- **A clinic:** your family doctor or GMF if you have one; otherwise the Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP) through Québec.ca or 811, or book a slot through Rendez-vous santé Québec.

Go to an ER promptly instead if you can't put any weight on the foot, the ankle looks deformed, the foot is numb, cold or pale, or the pain is severe. If you're unsure, call 811.

They aren't options for an adult's sprained ankle.

How to read the table: occupancy is how full the stretcher area is, and the waiting count is people present who haven't seen a physician yet, at every priority. The two often disagree (Lachine: 157% with 4 waiting; the CHUM: 145% with 42 waiting), and neither predicts your wait: MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner, since the numbers don't show staffing or triage and change quickly. That's why I'm not picking one for you; the nearest ER, or a non-ER route above, is a sound choice.
