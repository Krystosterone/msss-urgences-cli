**A 2-year-old with a 39.5 °C fever who is very drowsy needs to be seen now. Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room right away. Don't pick an ER by how busy it looks.**

- The nearest ER is the right one: general ERs treat children too, not only the two pediatric ones. If your child is hard to wake, isn't responding normally, has trouble breathing or has a seizure, call 911 rather than driving.
- ERs see children by how urgent their condition is, not in order of arrival, so a very drowsy child is assessed according to that wherever you go. The crowding data can't tell you which ER would see your child sooner, so it's not a reason to drive farther or to wait.
- If at any point you're unsure what to do, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, 24/7) can advise, but with a child this drowsy, don't hold off going while you decide.

For context only, here is what MSSS reported at its latest snapshot (taken 2026-11-05 at 08:00, published at 08:45) for Montréal's two pediatric ERs:

- CHU Sainte-Justine: 88% stretcher occupancy, 33 patients present, 9 waiting to see a physician.
- Montreal Children's: 183% stretcher occupancy, 48 patients present, 21 waiting to see a physician.

These are head counts at one moment, not wait times. MSSS itself warns that a lower count doesn't mean being seen sooner: the numbers don't show staffing or triage, and they change quickly. Please don't let them delay you: go to the closest ER now.
