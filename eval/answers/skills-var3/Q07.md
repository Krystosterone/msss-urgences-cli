**Not steadily. Over the five fiscal years that can be compared like for like, Montréal's ER strain indicators peaked in 2022-2023 and have eased since: by 2025-2026 they were back close to their 2021-2022 level. This year's figures can't be read as a trend yet, because fewer hospitals are in them.**

Montréal region, each fiscal year cumulated from April 1 to the end of period 7 (the same window every year), from MSSS's cumulative ER file:

| Fiscal year | ERs in the data | Mean stretcher stay | Stretcher stays 24 h+ | Stretcher stays 48 h+ | Mean delay to first physician |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 21 | 18.0 h | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2022-2023 | 21 | 19.9 h | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2023-2024 | 21 | 19.2 h | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2.7 h |
| 2024-2025 | 21 | 18.7 h | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2025-2026 | 21 | 18.4 h | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2026-2027 | 17 | 19.0 h | 23.9% | 7.3% | 2.7 h |

My reading, across those years (lower is better for every column):

- **2021-2022 → 2022-2023 got worse:** stays, the share of 24 h+ and 48 h+ stretcher stays, and the delay to a physician all rose.
- **2022-2023 → 2025-2026 improved each year** on the share of 24 h+ stays and the stretcher stay. By 2025-2026 the 24 h+ share and the delay to a physician were about where they stood in 2021-2022 (changes under a point or a quarter-hour count as stable), while the mean stretcher stay was still slightly longer.
- So over five comparable years: no lasting worsening, but no real improvement either. Overcrowding stayed high throughout, with roughly a quarter of stretcher patients staying 24 h or more.
- **2026-2027 so far is not comparable.** Its Montréal total covers 17 ERs against 21 in every earlier year (4 fewer), so a drop or rise in it can come from the missing hospitals rather than a real change. The live-data join lists 4 Montréal ERs with no 2026-2027 row (Sacré-Cœur, Fleury, Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost), most likely the same four. A like-for-like answer for this year would need a hospital-by-hospital comparison.

Right now, for context only (one snapshot isn't a trend): MSSS's Montréal stretcher occupancy rate is 161%, against 130% for Québec as a whole (snapshot taken 2026-11-05 at 08:00, published 08:45).

These are averages over months of past visits: they describe how Montréal's ERs have performed, not how long a visit today would take.

_MSSS cumulative ER file (Donnees_cumulatives_Urgence), fiscal years 2021-2022 to 2026-2027, each April 1 → period 7; MSSS hourly ER file for the live figures._
