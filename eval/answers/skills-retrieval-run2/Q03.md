**A 2-year-old who is very drowsy with a high fever needs to be seen now. Call 911, or go to the nearest emergency room right away. Please don't pick an ER based on how busy it looks.**

- Being very drowsy or hard to wake is a warning sign in a young child. If your child is hard to wake, has trouble breathing, has a seizure, or seems to be getting worse, call 911.
- Any ER can assess a child, and the nearest one is the right one. ERs see patients by how urgent their condition is, not in order of arrival, so the sickest children go first wherever they arrive.
- The crowding numbers can't tell you which ER would see your child sooner. Driving farther to a "less busy" ER only adds time.
- If you're unsure, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, 24/7) can advise you. With a very drowsy child, though, don't wait on hold: go now or call 911.

**For context only, not as a reason to choose one:** at the MSSS update published 2026-11-05 at 08:45, Montréal's two pediatric ERs reported:

- CHU Sainte-Justine: 88% stretcher occupancy, 9 children waiting to see a physician.
- Montreal Children's: 183% stretcher occupancy, 21 children waiting to see a physician.

These are head counts at one moment, not wait times. They don't show staffing or how sick the children already there are, and they change quickly.
