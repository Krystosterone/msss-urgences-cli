**Not clearly. Over the five fiscal years that can be compared, Montréal's ER strain peaked in 2022-2023 and then eased. By 2025-2026 it was roughly back to 2021-2022 levels, with stays a little longer and long stretcher stays slightly rarer. This year can't be compared yet, because fewer Montréal ERs are in the data.**

The cumulative MSSS file has no occupancy rate, so this uses its strain indicators instead. Each year covers the same window, April 1 to period 7 (roughly mid-October):

| Fiscal year | Montréal ERs in the data | Mean stretcher stay | Stretcher stays over 24 h | Stretcher stays over 48 h | Mean delay to first physician |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 21 ERs | 18.0 h | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2022-2023 | 21 ERs | 19.9 h | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2023-2024 | 21 ERs | 19.2 h | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2.7 h |
| 2024-2025 | 21 ERs | 18.7 h | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2025-2026 | 21 ERs | 18.4 h | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2026-2027 (so far) | 17 ERs | 19.0 h | 23.9% | 7.3% | 2.7 h |

- **2021-2022 to 2025-2026 (same number of ERs each year):** the worst year was 2022-2023 on every indicator. In 2025-2026, the mean stretcher stay (18.4 h vs 18.0 h) was slightly longer than in 2021-2022, while the share of stretcher stays over 24 h (24.6% vs 25.2%) and the mean delay to a physician were about the same. That isn't a worsening trend.
- **2026-2027 isn't comparable.** The data has 4 fewer Montréal ERs this year than last, so its averages and shares describe a different set of ERs. They're probably Sacré-Coeur, Fleury, Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost, which also have no row in this year's baseline. To confirm, I'd compare `urgences-cumulatif --region montreal --level facility` for both years.
- **Province, for context:** the share of stretcher stays over 24 h went from 20.9% in 2021-2022 to a peak of 23.5% in 2022-2023 and down to 20.3% in 2025-2026. This year's figure is also not comparable: the provincial total covers 108 ERs vs 116 last year, and Mauricie et Centre-du-Québec has no 2026-2027 data yet.
- **Occupancy itself:** Montréal's stretcher occupancy is 161% right now (MSSS's regional rate), but that's one snapshot. The hourly archive only goes back to 2026-11-03, so it can't show a trend in occupancy.

These are averages over months of past visits. They describe how Montréal's ERs have performed, not how long a visit today would take.

_Sources: MSSS cumulative ER file (Fichier cumulatif des données des urgences), fiscal 2026-2027 through period 7, compared with 2025-2026 and the four years before; MSSS hourly ER file published 2026-11-05 at 08:45._
