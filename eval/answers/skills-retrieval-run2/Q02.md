**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is much more crowded than Sainte-Justine's.** Here are the two side by side (MSSS hourly file, published 2026-11-05 at 08:45):

|---|---|---|

| Stretchers occupied / functional | 14 / 16 | 22 / 12 |
| Waiting to see a physician | 9 waiting | 21 waiting |
| Patients present | 33 present | 48 present |
| On a stretcher over 24 h | none over 24 h | 3 over 24 h |
| On a stretcher over 48 h | none over 48 h | 1 over 48 h |
| Gap from the mean of Montréal's ERs | Δ −64 | Δ +31 |
| Yesterday's mean stretcher stay vs this fiscal year | 6.6 h vs 6.4 h (+2%) | 10.3 h vs 8.8 h (+16%) |

**What each indicator means**

- **Stretcher occupancy** is occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers. It shows how full the stretcher area is. Over 100% is common at busy ERs, because MSSS counts patients on overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional. Higher means more crowded. It doesn't measure the waiting room or how fast anyone is seen.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't been assessed by a physician yet, at every triage priority. It isn't a place in line: the sickest children are seen first, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
- **Patients present** is everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on stretchers and walk-in.
- **Over 24 h / over 48 h** count children who have been on a stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Gap from the mean of Montréal's ERs** compares each with the plain mean of Montréal's reporting ERs (152%), which mixes adult and pediatric ERs. For these two, comparing them directly with each other means more. MSSS's own Montréal rate is 161%.
- **Yesterday's stretcher stay vs the fiscal year** (from `--baseline`) compares the mean stay of patients who left yesterday with the same ER's average since April 1 (fiscal 2026-2027, through period 7). Sainte-Justine's is normal for it. The Children's is somewhat longer than usual, and its walk-in stays are too (+16%). It describes yesterday's patients, not a child arriving today.

**What the numbers can't tell you:** how long your child would wait. They're head counts at one moment, and they change quickly. For an estimate, Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) gives MSSS's estimated wait per ER for a non-priority case. It depends on who is there when you arrive, doesn't apply to urgent cases, and isn't a promise. If you're deciding whether a child needs to come in at all, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7. If symptoms could be serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER. I can't say yet whether this morning is unusual for either hospital: the hourly history archive only began on 2026-11-03, which is too little to establish a norm.
