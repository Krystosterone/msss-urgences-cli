**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is much more crowded than Sainte-Justine's.**

- **CHU Sainte-Justine:** 88% stretcher occupancy (14 occupied of 16 functional stretchers), 9 children waiting to see a physician, 33 patients present, none on a stretcher for 24 h or more.
- **Montreal Children's (Glen site):** 183% occupancy (22 occupied of 12 functional), 21 waiting to see a physician, 48 present, 3 on a stretcher for 24 h or more, 1 of them for 48 h or more.
- **Context:** MSSS's Montréal rate is 161% and the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152%, adult ERs included. Sainte-Justine is below that mean (Δ −64), the Children's above it (Δ +31). Comparing the two pediatric ERs with each other is more meaningful than comparing either with the adult-dominated mean.
- **Against each ER's own norm** (fiscal year 2026-2027, April 1 → period 7): at Sainte-Justine, patients who left yesterday stayed 6.6 h on a stretcher vs 6.4 h on average this year (+2%: normal). At the Children's, 10.3 h vs 8.8 h (+16%: somewhat longer than usual), and walk-in patients' stays were also 16% longer (5.2 h vs 4.5 h).

**What the indicators mean**

- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied ÷ functional stretchers. It shows how full the stretcher area is. Going over 100% is common at busy ERs: MSSS counts overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional. It says nothing about the waiting room or how fast anyone is seen.
- **Waiting to see a physician** = patients present who haven't been assessed by a physician yet, at every triage priority. It's not a place in line: the sickest children are seen first wherever they arrive.
- **Patients present** = everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or not.
- **On a stretcher 24 h / 48 h or more** = mostly children waiting for a bed upstairs: a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Δ** = how many points an ER's occupancy is above or below the plain mean of Montréal's ERs (calculated by the CLI, not published by MSSS).
- **Yesterday's stay vs this year's** = mean time from arrival to departure for patients who left yesterday, set against the same ER's mean since April 1. A past average, not a forecast for anyone arriving now.

None of these is a wait time, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean a child will be seen sooner. Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows an estimated wait for non-priority cases per ER; it depends on the staff and patients present when you arrive, doesn't apply to urgent cases, and isn't a promise. If you're deciding whether to come in at all, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7; if a child's symptoms could be serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
