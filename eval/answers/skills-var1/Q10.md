**Yes, by its own yardstick: patients who left Maisonneuve-Rosemont's ER yesterday stayed noticeably longer than its norm for this fiscal year, even though its stretcher area is less full than most Montréal ERs this morning.**

- **Stretcher patients** who left yesterday stayed 29.4 h on average, vs 22.1 h so far in fiscal 2026-2027 (April 1 → period 7): +7.36 h, or +33%. That's noticeably longer than usual.
- **Walk-in (ambulatory) patients** who left yesterday stayed 7.8 h vs a norm of 5.8 h (+35%), also noticeably longer than usual.
- **Right now:** 133% stretcher occupancy (72 occupied of 54 functional stretchers), 30 people waiting to see a physician, 99 present, 31 on a stretcher for 24 h or more (8 of them for 48 h or more). That's below the mean of Montréal's ERs (152%; Δ −19) and MSSS's Montréal rate of 161%: busy, but not among the most crowded this morning. So it's "busier than it usually is" on stays while not unusually full right now.
- **Its norm is already heavy:** so far this fiscal year, 36.6% of its stretcher visits lasted 24 h or more, and its average stretcher stay (22.1 h) is 3.49 h longer than at the same point last year (18.6 h in 2025-2026).
- The hourly history can't add much yet: the archive began on 2026-11-03 at 22:45, so this morning's 133.3% can be set against only one earlier snapshot at the same hour (142.6%), too thin to judge.

Keep in mind that the stay figures are *yesterday's* averages for patients who already left, not today's and not the wait or stay for someone arriving now. Occupancy and head counts aren't wait times either.
