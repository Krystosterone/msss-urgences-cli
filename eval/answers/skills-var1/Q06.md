**The data can't tell you which ER would see you fastest, or how long you'd wait, and for a sprain you consider minor there are other routes to care worth trying first.**

**Other routes for a minor sprain:**

- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse can advise you by phone, 24/7, including whether you need to be seen today and where.
- **A pharmacist** can advise on minor problems and, for some conditions, prescribe.
- **Your own family doctor or GMF**, or, without one, the GAP (Québec.ca or 811). Rendez-vous santé Québec or a clinic's own booking system can find an actual appointment.

Go to an ER instead if you can't put any weight on the foot, the ankle looks deformed, the foot is numb, cold or pale, there's an open wound, or the pain is severe.

**How long you'd wait:** MSSS's open data has no wait time. Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows, for each ER, an estimated wait before seeing a physician for a non-priority case. It's an estimate that depends on the staff and patients present when you arrive, it doesn't apply to urgent cases, and it isn't a promise.

- Not reported right now: Hôpital du Sacré-Cœur, Hôpital Fleury and Hôpital Jean-Talon (that means no data, not calm or closed).
- Set apart because they serve specific patients: CHU Sainte-Justine and the Montreal Children's (children), Douglas and the Institut universitaire en santé mentale de Montréal (psychiatric), and the Institut de cardiologie de Montréal (cardiology).
- For the region as a whole, MSSS's Montréal rate is 161% (the mean of its ERs is 152%).

Occupancy measures how full the stretcher area is, not how fast walk-in patients like you are seen, and the counts of people waiting include every triage priority. The two can disagree: Lachine shows 157% occupancy with 4 people waiting, while the CHUM shows 145% with 42. MSSS itself warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner, so these numbers can't pick the fastest ER for you.
