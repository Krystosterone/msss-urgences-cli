**Not steadily. Across the last six fiscal years, Montréal's ER indicators have gone up and down rather than getting worse year after year: the worst year was 2022-2023, and the share of very long stretcher stays has improved every year since. This year is not quite comparable, though (see the caveat).**

Montréal region total, each year cumulated from April 1 to period 7 (the same window every year):

- **Stretcher stays of 24 h or more** (share of stretcher visits): 25.2% in 2021-2022, 27.3% in 2022-2023, 26.7% in 2023-2024, 25.9% in 2024-2025, 24.6% in 2025-2026, 23.9% in 2026-2027. Down every year since the 2022-2023 peak.
- **Average stretcher stay:** 18.0 h in 2021-2022, 19.9 h in 2022-2023, 19.2 h in 2023-2024, 18.7 h in 2024-2025, 18.4 h in 2025-2026, 19.0 h in 2026-2027. Up +0.55 h (about half an hour) on last year, but still below the 2022-2023 peak.
- **Average delay to first physician assessment** (all patients, from the end of triage): 2.6 h in 2021-2022, 2.8 h in 2022-2023, 2.7 h in 2023-2024, 2.8 h in 2024-2025, 2.6 h in 2025-2026, 2.7 h in 2026-2027. Roughly flat; +0.12 h on last year, which counts as stable.
- **Stretcher stays of 48 h or more:** 6.7% in 2021-2022, 9.2% in 2022-2023, 7.8% in 2023-2024, 7.2% in 2024-2025, 6.7% in 2025-2026, 7.3% in 2026-2027.

**Caveat on coverage:** this year's Montréal total covers 17 ERs, against 21 in every earlier year (change −4), so 2026-2027 isn't like-for-like and its raw counts, such as the 425,586 visits, can't be compared with earlier years. Four Montréal ERs have no 2026-2027 row in the cumulative file (Hôpital du Sacré-Cœur, Hôpital Fleury, Hôpital Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost), and they are probably the four missing from the total; they're also not reporting live data right now.

My reading: there's no clear long-run worsening; 2022-2023 was the worst year on every one of these measures, and the 24 h share is now the lowest of the six years. These are averages over months of past visits, not a measure of anyone's wait today. For right now, MSSS's Montréal rate is 161% stretcher occupancy, but one snapshot says nothing about a trend.
