**Several clinics in the Ahuntsic – Montréal-Nord RLS regularly offer non-urgent appointments through the provincial booking hub, mostly on weekday daytimes, and a few also in the evening and on weekends.**

What MSSS's clinic file shows, over the 7 most recent dates it has published (2026-10-22 to 2026-10-28). These are appointments that were offered on those days, not openings now:

- **Largest physician offer:** District Médical (426 physician appointments on 2026-10-28; 342 on Thursday 2026-10-22), Centre médical Mieux-Être succ. Ahuntsic (172 on 2026-10-28), District Médical Junior (143) and Clinique médicale Désy (113).
- **CLSC Ahuntsic** offered both physician (22) and nurse (38) appointments on 2026-10-28. Clinique médicale Désy also offered nurse appointments (46 that day). "Nurse" here covers nurses and specialized nurse practitioners.
- **Evenings (appointments scheduled 18h–21h):** District Médical offered physician slots in that band on every one of the 7 dates (25 on 2026-10-28). Clinique médicale Désy (11) and District Médical Junior (8) also did on 2026-10-28.
- **Weekends:** on Sunday 2026-10-25, District Médical (56), District Médical Junior (45) and CLSC Ahuntsic (20) offered physician appointments.
- More offers means a clinic puts more appointments on the hub, not that you're likelier to get one: the file doesn't say whether they were booked, some may be reserved for the clinic's own patients, and clinics outside the hub aren't counted.

**How to actually get seen for your ear:**

- **Find a real slot:** Rendez-vous santé Québec or the clinic's own booking system.
- **A pharmacist** can advise on minor problems and, for some conditions, prescribe.
- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse's advice by phone, 24/7, including whether this can wait.
- **Your own family doctor or GMF**, if you have one. Without a family doctor, the Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP), on Québec.ca or through 811; check Québec.ca for current eligibility.

Since you say it isn't urgent, these are the usual routes. Go to an ER (or call 911) instead if things change: a high fever, severe pain, swelling or redness behind the ear, a stiff neck or a severe headache, confusion, or sudden hearing loss or dizziness.
