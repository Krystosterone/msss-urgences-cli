**No. Finding no clinic appointment after 9 pm doesn't mean you need the ER — and the appointment data can't decide that for you.** Whether ear pain needs to be seen tonight is a medical question, and it doesn't depend on which slots happen to be listed.

What you can do tonight:
- **Call Info-Santé 811.** A nurse answers around the clock and can tell you whether this can wait for a clinic appointment tomorrow, or whether you should be seen tonight.
- **Ask a pharmacist** (if one near you is still open): they can advise on pain relief and, for some conditions, prescribe.
- **Tomorrow:** your own family doctor or GMF if you have one; otherwise the Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP), via Québec.ca or 811; or look for a slot on Rendez-vous santé Québec or a clinic's own booking system.

**Go to the ER, or call 911, without waiting for an appointment** if the pain becomes severe, or comes with a high fever, swelling or redness behind the ear, a stiff neck, confusion, sudden hearing loss, dizziness or weakness of the face — or whenever you feel something is seriously wrong.

**Context: late-night clinic appointments are rare in your area anyway.** In MSSS's clinic file for the Ahuntsic – Montréal-Nord local network, over the seven most recent published dates, the only clinic that offered any appointments scheduled after 21h was District Médical (physicians), and only a handful each day: 2 on 2026-10-22, 5 on 2026-10-23, 6 on 2026-10-24, 3 on 2026-10-25, 1 on 2026-10-26, 5 on 2026-10-27 and 3 on 2026-10-28. Several clinics did offer evening slots between 18h and 21h on weekdays. So an empty list after 9 pm is normal here; it says nothing about how serious your ear pain is.

These are appointments offered on past dates (the file is published weekly; its newest date is 2026-10-28), not what's free tonight, and they cover only clinics on the provincial booking hub.

_Source: MSSS "Rendez-vous en clinique" file, published weekly; dates 2026-10-22 to 2026-10-28._
