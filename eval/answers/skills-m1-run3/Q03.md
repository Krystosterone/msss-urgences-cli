**Please call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room now.** A 2-year-old with a 39.5 °C fever who is very drowsy needs to be seen right away; if your child is hard to wake, or has trouble breathing or a seizure, call 911.

- **Go to the nearest ER, whichever it is.** Other hospitals' ERs also treat children, and in an emergency the closest one is the right one. Don't drive farther, and don't wait, because one ER looks less busy.
- **The data can't tell you where your child will be seen faster.** ERs see children by how urgent their condition is, not in order of arrival, so a very drowsy child is a priority wherever you arrive. MSSS itself warns that a low count of patients doesn't mean being seen sooner.
- If you're unsure what to do on the way, Info-Santé 811 can advise, but don't let a call delay you if your child is getting worse.

For context only, since you asked (MSSS hourly file, published 2026-11-05T08:45): Sainte-Justine's stretcher area is at 88% occupancy with 9 children waiting to see a physician; the Montreal Children's is at 183% with 21 waiting. These are head counts at that moment, not wait times, and they can change quickly — they are not a reason to choose one hospital over the closer one.
