**The open data can't tell you which ER will see you fastest or how long you'd wait, so I can't pick one for you.** ERs see people by triage priority, not in order of arrival, and MSSS warns that a low count of patients doesn't mean being seen sooner.

**For how long you'd wait:** Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows, for each ER, an estimated wait before seeing a physician for a *non-priority* case, from MSSS's provincial ER console. It isn't in the data I have. It depends on the staff and the patients present when you arrive, it doesn't apply to urgent cases, and it isn't a promise.

**For a sprain you consider minor, the ER isn't your only option:**
- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse can advise you by phone, around the clock, on whether and where you should be seen.
- **A pharmacist** can advise on minor injuries and pain relief.
- **Your family doctor or GMF**, or, without one, the Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP) via Québec.ca or 811; Rendez-vous santé Québec lists clinic appointments.
- **Go to the ER (or call 911)** if you can't put any weight on the foot, the ankle looks deformed, the foot is numb, cold or pale, there's an open wound, or the pain and swelling are severe.
