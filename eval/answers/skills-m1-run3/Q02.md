**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is far more crowded than Sainte-Justine's: about twice as full on stretchers, with more than twice as many children waiting to see a physician.**

| | CHU Sainte-Justine | Montreal Children's |
|---|---|---|
| Stretcher occupancy | 88% (14 occupied / 16 functional) | 183% (22 occupied / 12 functional) |
| Waiting to see a physician | 9 waiting | 21 waiting |
| Total present | 33 present | 48 present |
| On a stretcher 24 h+ (48 h+) | 0 over 24 h (0 over 48 h) | 3 over 24 h (1 over 48 h) |
| Gap from the mean of Montréal's ERs | Δ -64 | Δ +31 |
| Yesterday's mean stretcher stay vs this fiscal year | 6.6 h vs 6.4 h (+2%) | 10.3 h vs 8.8 h (+16%) |

**What each indicator means**
- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers (derived from MSSS's counts). Over 100% is common at busy ERs, because MSSS counts patients on overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional. Higher means a more crowded stretcher area; it doesn't measure the waiting room or how fast anyone is seen.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't yet been assessed by a doctor, at every triage priority. It's not a place in line: the sickest children are seen first.
- **Total present** is everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or in the waiting room.
- **On a stretcher 24 h+ / 48 h+** counts children who have been on a stretcher that long right now, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Gap from the mean** compares each ER's occupancy with the plain mean of Montréal's reporting ERs (152%), which mixes adult and pediatric ERs, so the direct comparison between the two children's hospitals is the more meaningful one. Montréal's overall regional rate (MSSS's total) is 161%.
- **Stretcher stay vs this fiscal year**: the mean time on a stretcher for patients who left *yesterday*, against the same hospital's mean since April 1 (fiscal year 2026-2027, through period 7). Sainte-Justine is in line with its norm; the Children's was somewhat longer than usual yesterday (walk-in stays there were also +16%, 5.2 h vs 4.5 h). These are past averages, not the stay of a child arriving now.

**What these can't tell you:** none of these numbers is a wait time. MSSS warns that a low count of patients present or waiting doesn't mean being seen sooner — the figures don't show staffing or triage, and they change quickly. The archived history (which starts at 2026-11-03T22:45) is still too short to say whether either ER is unusual for this hour. If you're deciding whether a child needs to go in at all, Info-Santé 811 answers around the clock; if a child seems seriously ill, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
