**Yes, by its own yardstick: patients who left Maisonneuve-Rosemont's ER yesterday stayed noticeably longer than its average so far this fiscal year. Its stretcher occupancy right now, though, is below the mean of Montréal's ERs.**

**Against its own norm** (yesterday's stays vs fiscal year 2026-2027, April 1 → period 7)
- Stretcher patients who left yesterday stayed 29.4 h on average, against 22.1 h so far this fiscal year: +33%, noticeably longer than usual.
- Walk-in (ambulatory) patients stayed 7.8 h yesterday, against 5.8 h this year: +35%, also noticeably longer.
- For context, this year's norm is itself heavier than last year's: so far in 2026-2027, 36.6% of its stretcher visits lasted 24 h or more (28.5% at the same point of 2025-2026), and the mean stretcher stay is 22.1 h (vs 18.6 h).

**Right now** (MSSS hourly file, published 2026-11-05T08:45)
- 133% stretcher occupancy: 72 patients on 54 functional stretchers.
- 30 people waiting to see a physician; 31 on a stretcher for 24 h or more, 8 of them for 48 h or more.
- Montréal's regional rate is 161% and the mean of its reporting ERs is 152%, so Maisonneuve-Rosemont sits below that mean right now (Δ -19).
- Against the archived history for this hour, now (133.3%) reads -9.3 points from the typical 142.6%, but that "typical" rests on a single other snapshot (the archive only starts at 2026-11-03T22:45), so it's too thin to say anything.

**My reading:** busy by occupancy (though less full than many Montréal ERs right now), but stays yesterday were well above its norm for the year — a sign of strain, mostly patients waiting a long time on stretchers. Two limits: the "norm" comparison is about *yesterday's* departures, not today's, since MSSS's stay figures are previous-day; and none of these numbers is a wait time — patients are seen by triage priority. For an estimate of the wait for a non-priority case, see Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences).
