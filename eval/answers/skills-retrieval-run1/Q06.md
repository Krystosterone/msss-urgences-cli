**The open data can't tell you which ER would see you fastest or how long you'd wait, so I can't pick one for you. For an estimated wait, Québec.ca publishes one per ER.**

**How long would I wait?** MSSS's open data has no wait time. Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows, for each ER, an *estimated* wait before seeing a physician for a non-priority case, from MSSS's provincial ER console. It's only an estimate: it depends on the staff and the patients present when you arrive, it doesn't apply to urgent cases, and it isn't a promise.

**Before choosing an ER, consider whether you need one.** For a sprain you describe as minor:
- **Info-Santé 811** (a nurse, 24/7) can tell you whether it needs to be seen and where.
- **A pharmacist** can advise on minor problems.
- **Your own family doctor or GMF**, or without one the **GAP** (Québec.ca or 811); to find an actual clinic slot, **Rendez-vous santé Québec** or the clinic's own booking system.
- **Go to an ER** if you can't put any weight on the foot, the ankle looks deformed, the foot is numb, cold or changing colour, there's an open wound, or the pain and swelling are severe.

**Why these numbers don't answer "fastest":** occupancy measures how full the stretcher area is, and "waiting" is a head count of everyone not yet seen, at every priority level. MSSS itself warns that a low count of patients present or waiting doesn't mean you'll see a physician sooner: the numbers don't show staffing or triage, and they change quickly.
