**A 2-year-old with a 39.5 °C fever who is very drowsy needs to be seen now. If your child is hard to wake, limp, having trouble breathing, has a seizure, or you're worried, call 911. Otherwise go to the nearest emergency room now, whichever is closest to you, rather than choosing one by how busy it looks.** If you're unsure how serious it is, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, 24/7) can advise you right away, but don't let a call delay you if your child is getting harder to wake.

Why crowding shouldn't decide this: ERs see children by how urgent their condition is, not in arrival order, so a drowsy, feverish toddler is triaged ahead of less urgent cases wherever you arrive. The open data can't tell which ER will see your child sooner, and driving farther costs time. Any ER can assess and stabilize a child, not only the two pediatric ones.

Only as context, here is what MSSS reports for Montréal's two pediatric ERs right now:
- **CHU Sainte-Justine:** 88% stretcher occupancy, 9 children waiting to see a physician.
- **Montreal Children's (Glen site):** 183% stretcher occupancy, 21 children waiting to see a physician.

These are head counts at the snapshot, not wait times: they don't show staffing or how urgent each case is, and they can change within the hour. MSSS warns that a lower count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
