**Not steadily. Over the five comparable fiscal years, Montréal's ER strain indicators worsened in 2022-2023 and have eased since: by 2025-2026 they were back to roughly where they were in 2021-2022. This year's figures can't be compared directly, because they cover fewer ERs.**

What I used: MSSS's cumulative ER file, which has no stretcher-occupancy rate. So "overcrowding" here means its strain indicators: how long patients stay on an ER stretcher, the share who stay 24 h or 48 h or more, and the delay from triage to first seeing a physician. Every year is cumulated over the same window, **April 1 to period 7** (roughly mid-October), so the years compare like with like.

**Montréal region, April 1 → period 7 of each fiscal year**

| Fiscal year | ERs in the data | Mean stretcher stay | Stretcher stays ≥ 24 h | Stretcher stays ≥ 48 h | Mean delay to see a physician |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 21 ERs | 18.0 h | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2022-2023 | 21 ERs | 19.9 h | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2023-2024 | 21 ERs | 19.2 h | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2.7 h |
| 2024-2025 | 21 ERs | 18.7 h | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2.8 h |
| 2025-2026 | 21 ERs | 18.4 h | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2.6 h |
| 2026-2027 | 17 ERs | 19.0 h | 23.9% | 7.3% | 2.7 h |

(Lower is better in every column. All of these are averages and shares over months of past visits, not anyone's wait today.)

**Reading it**
- **2021-2022 → 2022-2023: worse.** The mean stretcher stay rose, and the shares of stretcher patients staying 24 h or more and 48 h or more both peaked.
- **2022-2023 → 2025-2026: better each year.** Stays shortened and the 24 h+ share fell below its 2021-2022 level. The mean delay to see a physician followed the same shape: up in 2022-2023, back near its 2021-2022 level by 2025-2026.
- So across these five years, the picture is a peak followed by a recovery, not a steady worsening.

**Why 2026-2027 is set apart:** the latest year has **17 ERs** in Montréal's total against **21 ERs** in every earlier year (a change of four facilities), so its averages and shares describe a different set of ERs and aren't comparable. The live ER file's baseline join lists **4 Montréal ERs** with no row this fiscal year (Sacré-Coeur, Fleury, Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost), which is likely, but not certainly, the same set. To confirm which ones dropped out, I'd run `quebec-sante urgences-cumulatif --region montreal --level facility --year 2026-2027` and the same for 2025-2026, and compare.

**What about occupancy?** Stretcher-occupancy history only comes from archived hourly snapshots, and that archive only started on **2026-11-03T22:45** (35 snapshots), so it can't show a trend over years. For today only: Montréal's rate (MSSS's regional total) is **161%** in the hourly file published 2026-11-05T08:45. One snapshot says nothing about a trend.

_Sources: MSSS cumulative ER file (Fichier cumulatif des données des urgences), fiscal years 2021-2022 to 2026-2027, each cumulated through period 7; MSSS hourly ER file published 2026-11-05T08:45 for the current rate._
