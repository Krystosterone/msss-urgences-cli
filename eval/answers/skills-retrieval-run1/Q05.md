**No. Not finding a clinic appointment tonight doesn't, by itself, mean you need the ER, and it doesn't mean you don't. Whether you need the ER depends on your symptoms, which is a medical call that appointment data can't make.**

**Who can help you decide tonight**
- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse, 24/7, who can tell you whether to go to the ER, see someone tomorrow, or manage at home.
- **A pharmacist:** can advise on minor problems and, for some conditions, prescribe.
- **Your own family doctor or GMF**, if you have one.
- **No family doctor?** The Guichet d'accès à la première ligne (GAP), at Québec.ca or through 811.
- **Booking:** Rendez-vous santé Québec or the clinic's own system, to look for an actual slot.

**If something could be serious, don't wait for an appointment:** trouble breathing, chest pain, signs of a stroke, heavy bleeding, a seizure, confusion or being very drowsy, or thoughts of suicide mean calling 911 or going to the nearest ER now.

**What MSSS's clinic data shows, as context.** It can't show tonight: it lists appointments clinics *offered* on past dates, published weekly (Thursdays at 14h), and its newest date is 2026-10-28. In the RLS d'Ahuntsic - Montréal-Nord, over 2026-10-22 to 2026-10-28 (data for all **7 dates**), late slots were rare: only District Médical offered physician appointments scheduled in the 21h–6h band, a handful per day (for example **3 on 2026-10-28** and **5 on 2026-10-27**). That's a sign the clinic sometimes books late appointments, not that it has one free tonight, and clinics outside the booking hub aren't counted at all.

_Source: MSSS "Portrait quotidien des rendez-vous en clinique", published weekly; data for 2026-10-22 to 2026-10-28._
