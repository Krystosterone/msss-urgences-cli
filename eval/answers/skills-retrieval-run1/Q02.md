For context, Montréal's overall rate (MSSS's regional total, adult ERs included) is **161%**.

**What each indicator means**
- **Stretcher occupancy** = occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers. It shows how full the stretcher area is. It routinely goes over 100% at busy ERs because MSSS counts overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional. Higher means more crowded; it says nothing about the waiting room or how fast a child is seen.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't yet been assessed by a doctor, at every triage level. It's not a place in line: the sickest children are seen first, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
- **Patients present** is everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or not.
- **Over 24 h / 48 h on a stretcher** counts children who have been on an ER stretcher that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs. It's a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Yesterday's stay vs its fiscal-year norm** compares the mean stretcher stay of patients who left yesterday with the same hospital's mean since April 1 (fiscal 2026-2027, through period 7). Within about ±10% is ordinary; +10% to +25% is "somewhat longer than usual". It describes yesterday's patients, not anyone arriving now.
- Each ER is also compared with "the mean of Montréal's ERs" (**152%**) in the data, but that mean mixes adult and pediatric ERs, so comparing the two children's hospitals directly is more meaningful.

**What these numbers can't tell you:** none of this is a wait time or predicts which ER would see a child sooner. For the official estimated wait for a non-urgent case, see Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences); it depends on who's there when you arrive and isn't a promise. If you're deciding whether a child needs the ER at all, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7; if symptoms could be serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
