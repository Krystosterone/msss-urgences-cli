**There's no sign that CHU Sainte-Justine's ER is unusually crowded right now. But the data can't yet say what's normal for a Thursday morning in particular.** I checked three views, and each answers a different question:

**Right now, compared with Montréal's other ERs (MSSS hourly file).**
- Sainte-Justine has 14 of its 16 functional stretchers occupied, which is 88% occupancy.
- 33 patients are present, 9 of them waiting to see a physician. Nobody has been on a stretcher for more than 24 h.
- That's well below Montréal's rate of 161% (MSSS's regional total) and below the 152% mean of Montréal's ERs. That mean mixes adult and pediatric ERs. Montréal's other pediatric ER, the Montreal Children's, is at 183%.

**Compared with its own norm this fiscal year (baseline).**
- Patients who left yesterday spent 6.6 h on a stretcher on average. That's 2% more than its mean of 6.4 h since April 1 (fiscal year 2026-2027, through period 7).
- Walk-in (ambulatory) patients stayed 4.3 h on average, against a norm of 4.1 h, which is 3% more.
- Both are within normal day-to-day variation, so it's in line with its usual. These are yesterday's stays, not a prediction for a child arriving now.

**Compared with the same hour in the archived history: not possible yet.**
- The history archive only started on 2026-11-03T22:45 and holds 35 hourly snapshots.
- The slot for around 8h has 2 samples. Its "typical" occupancy for that hour (68.8%) therefore rests on just 1 other snapshot, and the archive contains no other Thursday.
- That's far too little to say whether 88% is high or low for a Thursday morning.

**My reading:** the stretcher area isn't crowded. Sainte-Justine is calmer than most of Montréal right now and in line with its own norm for the year. Telling whether this is unusual for a Thursday morning will take several more weeks of history.

These counts are not wait times. Children are seen in order of how urgent their condition is, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner. If you're unsure whether to bring a child in, Info-Santé 811 answers around the clock. If the symptoms could be serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
