**A 2-year-old who is very drowsy with a fever needs to be seen now. Call 911, or go to the nearest emergency room right away.** Don't pick an ER based on how busy it is, and don't wait for a quieter moment. If your child is hard to wake, has trouble breathing, has a seizure, or is getting worse, call 911. If you're unsure what to do, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, available around the clock) can advise you immediately. But with a very drowsy child, don't let a phone call delay going in.

**Why crowding shouldn't decide this:** ERs see children by how urgent their condition is, not by arrival order or by how full the ER is. A child who seems seriously ill is seen first at either hospital. The data can't tell which ER would see your child sooner, and any ER, including a non-pediatric one, can assess and stabilize a sick child. The nearest ER is the right one.

For context only, not as a reason to go farther, this is the MSSS snapshot for Montréal's two pediatric ERs:
- **CHU Sainte-Justine:** 88% stretcher occupancy, 9 children waiting to see a physician, 33 present.
- **Montreal Children's:** 183% stretcher occupancy, 21 waiting, 48 present.

These are head counts at one moment, not wait times. MSSS warns that a lower count doesn't mean being seen sooner, and the numbers can change quickly.
