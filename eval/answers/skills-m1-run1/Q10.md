**Yes, by its own standard. Patients who left Hôpital Maisonneuve-Rosemont's ER yesterday stayed noticeably longer than its average so far this fiscal year. Right now, though, its stretcher area is less full than most of Montréal's.** Each of the views below answers a different question:

**Compared with its own norm (fiscal year 2026-2027, April 1 → period 7):**
- **Stretcher patients:** those who left yesterday stayed 29.4 h on average, against 22.1 h on average this fiscal year. That's 33% longer, noticeably longer than usual.
- **Walk-in (ambulatory) patients:** 7.8 h yesterday against a norm of 5.8 h, which is 35% longer. Also noticeably longer than usual.
- These are *yesterday's* completed stays, the most recent day MSSS publishes, not the stay of someone arriving today.

**Right now (MSSS hourly snapshot):**
- 72 patients occupy its 54 functional stretchers, which is 133% occupancy. 99 patients are present and 30 are waiting to see a physician.
- 31 patients have been on a stretcher for over 24 h, 8 of them for over 48 h. Most are probably waiting for a hospital bed, which is a sign of strain.
- Montréal's rate is 161% (MSSS's regional total), and the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152%. So Maisonneuve-Rosemont is busy, but less crowded than the Montréal average right now.

**Compared with the same hour in the archived history: too thin to tell.** In the archive, its occupancy around 8h this morning was 133.3%, against a "typical" 142.6% for that hour. That typical value rests on only 1 other snapshot (that hour has 2 samples, including this one), because the archive started on 2026-11-03T22:45. That isn't enough to call it calmer or busier than usual for the hour.

**Context only (no live equivalent):**
- So far this fiscal year, 36.6% of its stretcher visits lasted 24 h or more.
- Its average delay from triage to a first physician assessment has been 4.4 h. That's an average over every patient since April 1, not anyone's expected wait.
- Its norm is itself higher than last year's: the average stretcher stay so far in 2026-2027 is 22.1 h, against 18.6 h over the same months of 2025-2026.

These figures describe the ER's strain. They can't tell you how long a visit would take. For an official estimate for non-urgent cases, see Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences). If symptoms could be serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER. If you're unsure, call Info-Santé 811.
