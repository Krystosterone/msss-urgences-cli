For context, Montréal's overall rate is 161% (MSSS's regional total), and the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152%. Both figures mix adult and pediatric ERs, so comparing the two children's hospitals directly is more meaningful.

**What each indicator means**
- **Stretcher occupancy** is occupied stretchers ÷ functional stretchers. It shows how full the stretcher area is. It often goes above 100% because MSSS counts patients on overflow stretchers as occupied without counting those stretchers as functional. It doesn't measure the waiting room or how fast anyone is seen.
- **Patients present** is everyone in the ER at the snapshot, on a stretcher or in the waiting area.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't been assessed by a doctor yet, at every triage level. It isn't a place in line: the sickest children are seen first. MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
- **Over 24 h / 48 h on a stretcher** counts children who have been on a stretcher that long right now. Most of them are waiting for a bed upstairs, so it's a sign of strain in the hospital, not a measure of the wait to be seen.
- **Mean stretcher stay** is the average time from arrival to departure for stretcher patients who left *yesterday*. It's a past average, not a prediction.

**Compared with each hospital's own norm** (fiscal year 2026-2027, April 1 → period 7):
- Sainte-Justine's stay yesterday was 2% longer than its usual 6.4 h. That's normal day-to-day variation.
- The Children's stay was 16% longer than its usual 8.8 h, which is somewhat longer than usual.

**What these numbers can't tell you:** how long a particular child would wait. They're head counts at one moment, they don't show staffing, and they can change quickly. Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) gives an official estimated wait for non-urgent cases. If you're wondering whether a child needs the ER at all, Info-Santé 811 can advise. If symptoms could be serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.
