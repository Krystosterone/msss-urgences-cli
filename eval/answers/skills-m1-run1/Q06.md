**The open data can't tell you which ER will see you fastest or how long you'd wait, and I can't estimate it from these numbers.** For a wait estimate, Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences) shows, for each ER, MSSS's estimated wait before seeing a physician for a *non-priority* case. That estimate depends on the staff and patients present when you arrive, doesn't apply to urgent cases, and isn't a promise.

**For a sprain you may not need an ER at all.** These routes are often quicker:
- **Info-Santé 811:** a nurse, available day and night, can tell you whether the ankle needs the ER, a clinic or home care.
- **A pharmacist** can advise on minor injuries.
- **A clinic appointment:** your own family doctor or GMF. Without one, the GAP (Québec.ca or 811) can direct you. Book through Rendez-vous santé Québec or the clinic.

**Go to the ER (or call 911) instead if** you can't put any weight on the foot, the ankle looks deformed, the foot is numb, cold or pale, or the pain or swelling is severe and getting worse.
