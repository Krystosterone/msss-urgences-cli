**Not steadily.** On the strain indicators MSSS publishes, Montréal's ERs got worse in 2022-2023 and then improved every year through 2025-2026. They ended close to where they were in 2021-2022. This fiscal year's figures can't be compared with earlier years yet, because fewer Montréal ERs are in the data.

**What I used:** the MSSS cumulative ER file. It has no occupancy rate, so I used its strain indicators instead. Each year is cumulated over the same window, April 1 → period 7 (roughly mid-October).

| Fiscal year | Mean stretcher stay | Stretcher stays over 24 h | Stretcher stays over 48 h | Mean delay to first physician (all patients) | ERs in the data |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2022 | 18.0 h | 25.2% | 6.7% | 2.6 h | 21 |
| 2022-2023 | 19.9 h | 27.3% | 9.2% | 2.8 h | 21 |
| 2023-2024 | 19.2 h | 26.7% | 7.8% | 2.7 h | 21 |
| 2024-2025 | 18.7 h | 25.9% | 7.2% | 2.8 h | 21 |
| 2025-2026 | 18.4 h | 24.6% | 6.7% | 2.6 h | 21 |
| 2026-2027 | 19.0 h | 23.9% | 7.3% | 2.7 h | 17 |

**Reading it** (lower is better on every column):
- **2021-2022 to 2025-2026** is comparable: the same number of ERs each year, over five years.
  - 2022-2023 was the worst year: the longest stays, and the largest shares of stays over 24 h and over 48 h.
  - Each year after that improved.
  - By 2025-2026, the stay (18.4 h vs 18.0 h) was slightly longer than in 2021-2022, the share over 24 h was slightly lower, and the share over 48 h and the delay to a physician were back to the same level.
  - So over five years: a spike, then a return to roughly the 2021-2022 level, not a steady worsening.
- **2026-2027 so far is not comparable.** It covers 17 ERs, against 21 in 2025-2026, so its totals reflect which ERs are in the data as much as how they performed. The live ER file lists 4 Montréal ERs with no 2026-2027 row in this file: Hôpital du Sacré-Cœur-de-Montréal, Hôpital Fleury, Hôpital Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost. They are probably the missing ones, but this file doesn't name them. None of the four is reporting live data right now either.
- These are averages over months of past visits. They describe how the ERs performed, not how long a visit would take today.

**Occupancy history isn't deep enough for a trend.** The hourly occupancy archive only began on 2026-11-03T22:45, so it covers days, not years. Today's Montréal stretcher occupancy rate of 161% is a single snapshot (published 2026-11-05T08:45).

_Source: MSSS cumulative ER file (Donnees_cumulatives_Urgence), fiscal years 2021-2022 to 2026-2027, each April 1 → period 7, compared with 2025-2026 as the baseline year. The live figure comes from the MSSS hourly ER file._
