**A very drowsy child with a fever needs to be seen now: call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room right away.** If your child is hard to wake, having trouble breathing or having a seizure, call 911 rather than driving. Don't pick an ER by how busy it looks, and don't wait for a quieter time.

- **The nearest ER is the right one.** Every ER can assess and stabilize a child, not only the two pediatric ones, and it can arrange a transfer if needed.
- **Crowding can't tell you where your child would be seen faster.** ERs see patients by triage priority, so the most serious cases go first wherever they arrive. A very drowsy toddler is assessed on that basis, not behind the people already in the waiting room.
- If you have any doubt on the way, Info-Santé 811 (a nurse, 24/7) can advise, but don't let a phone call delay going in.

For context only, since you asked (this must not decide where you go): at the latest MSSS snapshot, CHU Sainte-Justine was at 88% stretcher occupancy with 9 children waiting to see a physician, and the Montreal Children's at 183% with 21 waiting and 3 on a stretcher for 24 h or more. These are head counts, not wait times: MSSS warns that a lower count doesn't mean being seen sooner, and the numbers change quickly.
