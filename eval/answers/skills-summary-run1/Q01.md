**No: nothing in the data says CHU Sainte-Justine's ER is unusually crowded this morning.** It is one of the least crowded ERs in Montréal right now, and yesterday's stays were in line with its norm for this year. What the data can't say yet is how this compares with *other Thursday mornings*: the history doesn't go back far enough.

Three views, each answering a different question:

- **Right now (live MSSS file).** 88% stretcher occupancy (14 of 16 functional stretchers occupied), 9 children waiting to see a physician, 33 present in total, and none on a stretcher for 24 h or more. Montréal's rate is 161% (MSSS's regional total) and the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152%; Sainte-Justine sits 64 points below that mean. That mean mixes adult and pediatric ERs; for comparison, the Montreal Children's ER is at 183%.
- **Against its own norm this fiscal year.** Patients who left yesterday spent 6.6 h on a stretcher on average, 2% more than its 6.4 h mean since April 1 (fiscal year 2026-2027, through period 7). That is within normal day-to-day noise. Walk-in (ambulatory) stays were +3%, also normal.
- **Against its own history at this hour: too thin to judge.** The hourly archive only starts on 2026-11-03 at 22:45 (35 snapshots so far), so it holds no earlier Thursday morning, and only one other snapshot around 8h (68.8% that time). The +18.7-point gap from that single snapshot is not a pattern.

What these numbers can't tell you: occupancy and head counts are not a wait time. Children are seen by how urgent their condition is, not in arrival order, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner. For the official estimated wait for a non-priority case, see Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences). If your child seems seriously ill, call 911 or go to the nearest ER; if you're unsure, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7.
