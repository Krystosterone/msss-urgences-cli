**Right now the Montreal Children's ER is much more crowded than Sainte-Justine's.** Neither number tells you how fast a child would be seen there.

**CHU Sainte-Justine**
- Stretcher occupancy: 88% (14 occupied on 16 functional stretchers).
- Waiting to see a physician: 9 children; 33 children present in total.
- On a stretcher for 24 h or more: 0 (none for 48 h or more either).
- Yesterday's mean stretcher stay: 6.6 h, 2% above its 6.4 h mean this fiscal year (normal).

**L'Hôpital de Montréal pour enfants (Montreal Children's, Glen site)**
- Stretcher occupancy: 183% (22 occupied on 12 functional stretchers).
- Waiting to see a physician: 21 children; 48 present in total.
- On a stretcher for 24 h or more: 3, of whom 1 for 48 h or more.
- Yesterday's mean stretcher stay: 10.3 h, 16% above its 8.8 h mean this fiscal year (somewhat longer than usual).

**What the indicators mean**
- **Stretcher occupancy** = stretchers occupied ÷ functional stretchers. It measures how full the stretcher area is. Over 100% is common at busy ERs: MSSS counts overflow stretchers as occupied but not as functional. It doesn't measure the waiting room or how fast anyone is seen.
- **Waiting to see a physician** counts children present who haven't seen a doctor yet, at every triage priority. It's not a place in line: the sickest children are seen first, and MSSS warns that a low count doesn't mean being seen sooner.
- **Total present** is every patient in the ER at the snapshot, seen or not.
- **On a stretcher 24 h / 48 h or more** are children who have been there that long, mostly waiting for a bed upstairs: a sign of strain on the hospital, not of the wait to be seen.
- **Yesterday's mean stretcher stay vs the fiscal-year mean** shows whether yesterday was a typical day *for that hospital*. Within about ±10% is normal noise; +10% to +25% is somewhat longer than usual. It's a past average, not a prediction for anyone arriving now.
- **Compared with Montréal:** the Montréal rate is 161% (MSSS's regional total) and the mean of Montréal's ERs is 152%; Sainte-Justine is 64 points below that mean and the Children's 31 above. That mean mixes adult and pediatric ERs, so comparing the two pediatric ERs directly is more meaningful.
- **These numbers move quickly.** In the short history archived so far (35 hourly snapshots since 2026-11-03 at 22:45), Sainte-Justine ranged from 68.8% to 162.5% and the Children's from 75% to 208.3%. That is too little history to say what's normal for this hour.

If you're deciding whether a child needs to come in at all, Info-Santé 811 answers 24/7; for anything that looks serious, call 911 or go to the nearest ER. For an official estimated wait for non-priority cases, see Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences).
