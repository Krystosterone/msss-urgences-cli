**Not steadily. On the fiscal-year indicators MSSS publishes, Montréal's ER strain rose to a peak in 2022-2023 and then eased, and by 2025-2026 it was roughly back to its 2021-2022 level (stretcher stays slightly longer, long-stay shares about the same).** The current fiscal year can't be compared like for like yet, because 4 fewer Montréal ERs are in this year's data.

**What I used.** MSSS's cumulative ER file has no stretcher-occupancy rate, so "overcrowding" here means its strain indicators. Every year is cumulated over the same window, April 1 → period 7 (roughly mid-October). I compared 5 comparable fiscal years, each covering the same number of Montréal ERs (21). Lower is better on all four.

- **Mean stay on a stretcher:** 18 h 02 (2021-2022), 19 h 53 (2022-2023), 19 h 13 (2023-2024), 18 h 43 (2024-2025), 18 h 26 (2025-2026).
- **Share of stretcher visits lasting 24 h or more:** 25.2% (2021-2022), 27.3% (2022-2023), 26.7% (2023-2024), 25.9% (2024-2025), 24.6% (2025-2026).
- **Share lasting 48 h or more:** 6.7% (2021-2022), 9.2% (2022-2023), 7.8% (2023-2024), 7.2% (2024-2025), 6.7% (2025-2026).
- **Mean delay from triage to first physician, all patients:** 2 h 34 (2021-2022), 2 h 50 (2022-2023), 2 h 41 (2023-2024), 2 h 45 (2024-2025), 2 h 35 (2025-2026).

**This fiscal year (2026-2027, so far):** stretcher stay 19 h 00, share on a stretcher 24 h or more 23.9%, 48 h or more 7.3%, delay to a physician 2 h 42. But there are 17 ERs in the data this year against 21 last year, so these averages describe a different set of ERs, and the CLI's year-over-year changes (for example +0.55 h of stretcher stay) are not comparable. The output doesn't name the ERs that dropped out. Sacré-Coeur, Fleury, Jean-Talon and Albert-Prévost have no row this fiscal year in the live ER join, which suggests they're the ones, but I can't confirm it from this data.

**What this can't show.** These are averages over months of past visits, not anyone's wait. Occupancy over time only exists in the hourly archive, which starts on 2026-11-03, far too short for a trend. This morning's Montréal rate of 161% (MSSS's regional total) is one snapshot, not a trend.

_Sources: MSSS cumulative ER file ("Fichier cumulatif des données des urgences"), fiscal year 2026-2027 through period 7, with every year back to 2021-2022 over the same April 1 → period 7 window; MSSS hourly ER file published 2026-11-05 at 08:45; quebec-sante history archive._
