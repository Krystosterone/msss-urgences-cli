**There isn't enough history yet to say when Verdun's ER is usually least crowded.** The archive of hourly snapshots only starts on 2026-11-03 at 22:45, so it holds 35 snapshots, about a day and a half, with just 1 or 2 snapshots for any given hour. That's too thin to call a pattern; with fewer than about 3 per hour, any "usual" time is guesswork.

What the little history there is shows (Québec local time, stretcher occupancy):
- The lowest hourly means were in the late evening: around 22h, 90.4% (2 snapshots, but one of them reached 107.7%); around 20h, 100% (a single snapshot); around 23h, 100% (2 snapshots, up to 115.4%).
- The fullest stretch was overnight into the morning: around 3h to 8h, means of about 125% to 128.8%.
- Over all 35 snapshots, Verdun ranged from 73.1% to 134.6%, averaging 115.7%.

Treat this as a day and a half of observations, not a schedule. It describes past crowding, not shorter waits, and it's never a reason to put off care if you might need it urgently.

**Right now:** Verdun is at 135% stretcher occupancy (35 occupied on 26 functional stretchers), 14 people are waiting to see a physician and 4 are on a stretcher for 24 h or more. Montréal's rate is 161% (MSSS's regional total). Patients who left yesterday stayed 16.6 h on a stretcher on average, in line with its 16.7 h mean so far this fiscal year (0% difference). None of this is a wait time: people are seen by triage priority. For an official estimate for non-priority cases, see Québec.ca's "Situation dans les urgences" page (quebec.ca/SituationUrgences).

To answer "when is it usually calmer?" properly, the same question needs asking again once the archive has a few weeks of snapshots, ideally by weekday and hour.
